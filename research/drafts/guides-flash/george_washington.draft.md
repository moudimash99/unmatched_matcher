## George Washington

**In short:** A tricky spymaster who controls the board with three 1-health Culper Spies and uses ruse tokens to force mind games. Opponents must guess whether an attack carries a real ruse effect or a bluff, risking a random card discard or a canceled defense.

### Plan A: Play the ruse mind game
**Goal:** Force your opponent into bad defensive choices and strip cards from their hand.
**How to do it:** You start with four ruse tokens. Before you attack, place a token next to your card. If your opponent wants to remove it before choosing defense, they must discard a random card. To keep them guessing, occasionally place a ruse token without a ruse effect or bluff with cards that don't need tokens, keeping opponents in a constant state of doubt. Save your actual ruse tokens for high-impact turns, or use a ruse on a high-value attack as your last action: if they feint, they take damage, and if they don't, you can disengage with movement. Use Misinformation to regain ruse tokens after winning combats.
**Watch out for:** Ruse recursion is easier to pull off if you time your Misinformation cards well, but relying too heavily on token generation can leave you vulnerable if you draw poor card combinations.

### Plan B: Keep the Culper Spies alive
**Goal:** Maximize network bonuses and map control while protecting your fragile sidekicks.
**How to do it:** Spread your three 1-health spies across different zones to protect them from area-of-effect damage. Keep them alive because you only have two cards that bring them back. Use Network Of Spies to add +1 to its value for each friendly fighter in Washington's zone, and use Sympathetic Stain to look at an opponent's hand and force a discard when a spy is adjacent to their fighter. Delay using the Undercover Agent defense swap early in the game if you still need your spies positioned safely across the board.
**Watch out for:** AoE attacks and area damage easily wipe out 1-health sidekicks if they are clustered together.

### How they lose
- You fail to land key interactions consistently, such as getting Circumvent to land into high blocks or high attacks into feints, leaving you short on damage.
- You are forced into a fatigue game against opponents stronger in exhaustion, particularly when you have to waste actions chasing them down.
- Your spies are eliminated early, depriving you of zone bonuses and reliable spy-based schemes.

### Key cards
- **Circumvent**: ignores the value of your opponent's card, making it vital for bypassing massive defenses.
- **Lead Astray**: a high-value attack that lets you move a fighter in the combat up to two spaces.
- **Agent 711**: deals heavy damage if you win the combat.
- **Gather Information**: draws three cards and lets your fighters move up to three spaces through opposing fighters.
- **Recruit To The Ring**: brings back a defeated spy, or places them anywhere on the board if you win the combat.

### Tips
- Mix up your attacks so opponents cannot safely ignore your ruse tokens or save all their feints for obvious moments.
- Against fighters stronger in exhaustion, be mindful that George functions mostly as a defensive fighter with limited passes.
- Do not waste your limited spy-recovery cards when spies can be safely repositioned or kept out of harms way.
