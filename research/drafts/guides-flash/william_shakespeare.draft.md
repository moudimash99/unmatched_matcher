## William Shakespeare

**In short:** A complex melee fighter who commands three fragile Actor sidekicks and plays a meticulous game of verse construction. By balancing syllable counts on attack and defense, he unlocks devastating completion effects like extra actions, unblockable damage, and massive healing.

### Plan A: Control the verse for heavy turns
**Goal:** Land high-impact completion effects, especially gaining extra actions or dealing automatic damage.
**How to do it:** Keep a strict mental count of the syllables on every card you play in combat. My Kingdom For a Horse (value 5, 6 syllables) grants 2 actions upon completion, essentially giving you two turns back-to-back. Pair it after a 4-syllable setup like All Are Punished or Et Tu, Brute? to catch the opponent off guard. For finishing power, Horror (value 4, 2 syllables) increases its attack value for each Horror in your line and deals unblockable damage equal to the number of other cards in your line upon completion.
**Watch out for:** Opponents will track your line's syllable count just as closely as you do. When you sit at 4 syllables, they know My Kingdom For a Horse is threatening and will play around it or pressure you into breaking the sequence.

### Plan B: Rebuild and survive with the Actors
**Goal:** Use your three Actor sidekicks as a defensive shield while recycling resources and recovering health.
**How to do it:** The Actors soak damage so Shakespeare stays safe, but they also wield potent cards. Such Sweet Sorrow lets an Actor move 2 spaces after combat, while Again returns the first card in your line to your hand. When Shakespeare takes a beating, Alas (value 2, 2 syllables) is your lifeline: losing the combat recovers 1 health, and completing the line draws 2 cards and recovers 2 more health. If Actors fall, Once More Unto the Breach (6 syllables) brings back a defeated Actor, or all of them upon completion.
**Watch out for:** Actors have only 1 health each, and area attacks or cards like All Are Punished can accidentally wipe out your own sidekicks if you aren't careful with positioning.

### How they lose
- Ranged fighters and elusive characters (such as Ghost Rider, Bullseye, and Sherlock Holmes) kite him endlessly, making it difficult to maintain initiative or complete lines.
- Heavy hitters with massive printed attacks overwhelm his lower defense values and punish him when he steps adjacent.
- Disruption and constant pressure force him to play cards suboptimally, ruining carefully planned syllable counts.
- Running low on card draw leaves him vulnerable unless he successfully triggers healing and drawing effects like Alas.

### Key cards
- **My Kingdom For a Horse**: A 5-value attack that grants 2 actions upon line completion, enabling explosive multi-action turns.
- **Horror**: A 5-copy 4-value attack that scales up in power and delivers unblockable automatic ping damage on completion.
- **Alas**: A vital versatile card that provides much-needed healing and card draw when completing your verse.
- **Deceive**: A 2-value versatile with a 2-syllable count that cancels all effects on the opponent's card and returns to your hand upon completion.
- **Again**: An Actor versatile that lets you pull the first card from your line back to your hand to correct your syllable count or reuse a key card.

### Tips
- Use 2-syllable cards like Horror and Deceive as flexible glue to hit exactly 10 syllables when larger cards would push you over.
- Remember that completion effects resolve immediately after combat ends—even during your opponent's turn—and cannot be canceled by Feints.
- Smaller maps like the Globe Theatre or Raptor Paddock suit him well, as his move-2 melee profile benefits from tight spaces where he can reach opponents without over-boosting.
- Avoid relying heavily on The Ides of March for combat value alone, as it acts as a blank 2 if not played for its completion effect.
