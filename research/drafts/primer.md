# Unmatched strategy primer

Winning games of Unmatched requires balancing your card resources, controlling the flow of initiative, and anticipating your opponent's moves through careful deck knowledge and tactical execution, ultimately reducing their hero's health to zero or surviving until exhaustion.

---

## Card advantage and hand size

**In short:** Maintaining a larger hand size gives you more options for offense and defense, but holding too many cards can force wasteful maneuvers or discarding.

* Your hand can hold up to 7 cards. Having more cards than your opponent gives you an advantage because your hand is more versatile, containing a balanced mix of attacks and defenses.
* When your hand approaches the maximum limit, you should go on the offensive rather than saving your best cards, since ending your turn near the limit leaves you vulnerable to being forced to boost or maneuver inefficiently.
* Conversely, holding a very small hand leaves you vulnerable to being overwhelmed, as you lack the defensive resources to respond to attacks. Balancing when to draw and when to spend cards is essential for card management.

**Term:** Card Advantage

**Sources:** Unmatched: basic strategy guide, Basic strategy, Tips - How to play aggro?, How to improve at the game ?

## Tempo and initiative

**In short:** Initiative determines who must approach and drive the action, while tempo dictates the rhythm and control of the exchanges.

* Initiative is held by the aggressor, the fighter who must actively approach and attack first. Holding it means you set the pace and your opponent has to answer you.
* The player without initiative can counter-pressure, boost away to safety, or ride the wave by double maneuvering to let the aggressor gas out. 
* Tempo represents the exchange and control of initiative. Tempo-based fighters can attack aggressively and then use movement or specific cards to force their opponent back into taking the initiative, punishing them for being defensive.

**Term:** Tempo

**Sources:** Basic strategy, UNMATCHED Advanced Fundamentals 1: tempo and initiative

## Trading damage and attacking vs defending

**In short:** Every attack carries an opportunity cost and potential consequences, making it vital to choose whether to press an attack or preserve your hand for defense.

* When you approach and attack, you must evaluate the consequences rather than just the immediate damage. Ending your turn adjacent to an opponent without sufficient defense leaves you exposed to a counter-attack.
* Unmatched features multiple action combinations, such as attacking twice or attacking and moving away. When an opponent attacks twice, the first attack is often a setup or bait for a stronger second strike.
* Using weaker attacks or versatile cards like Regroup can bait out your opponent's valuable defense cards, preserving your heavier hitters for later in the match.

**Term:** Combat Exchange

**Sources:** Unmatched: basic strategy guide, Basic strategy, UNMATCHED Advanced Fundamentals 1: tempo and initiative

## Counting cards

**In short:** Tracking public information like discard piles and key cards allows you to play around defensive cancels and unpredictable effects.

* Since each deck is known in advance and discards are public, checking your opponent's discard pile lets you see which key cards have already been played, such as how many Feints or cancels remain.
* Feints cancel all of your opponent's card effects for that combat, making them powerful defensive tools. Knowing when your opponent is out of Feints lets you commit your biggest attacks and effects safely.
* Card tracking extends to knowing your own deck and your opponent's deck in advance, ensuring you are never surprised by unexpected combat values or unique event cards.

**Term:** Card Counting

**Sources:** Unmatched: basic strategy guide, Basic strategy, An Unmatched Roster: Messy Politics

## Boosting

**In short:** Discarding a card for its boost value moves you further or makes an attack stronger, at the price of a card.

* Every card has a boost value. When you maneuver, you may discard one card to add its boost value to your movement. Some attack cards (and some fighters' abilities) let you boost the attack too, adding the boost card's value to the attack.
* A boost costs a whole card, so boost with the cards you need least: a card for a dead sidekick, a weak card for the matchup, or a duplicate. A boost card with a high boost value and a weak effect is ideal fuel.
* A Feint on a boosted attack cancels the boost, and the boost card is wasted. Against an opponent who still holds Feints, boost with cards you don't mind losing, or bait the Feints out first.

**Term:** Boosting

**Sources:** Unmatched: basic strategy guide, How to Play Unmatched: A Comprehensive Guide, An Unmatched Roster: Messy Politics

## Positioning, range, and zones

**In short:** The colored zones and board layout dictate whether melee or ranged fighters hold the advantage, making thoughtful movement essential.

* Each map is split into colored zones. A ranged fighter can attack anyone who shares a zone with them, however far away; a melee fighter has to stand next to the target.
* Smaller maps feature tight movement centers that favor aggressive melee fighters who want to close the distance quickly, while larger maps provide room for ranged fighters and characters with high mobility to kite opponents.
* Sidekicks and terrain features like doorways or item tokens can be used to body block bottlenecks, restrict enemy movement, or force opponents to waste boosts just to reach you.

**Term:** Map Positioning

**Sources:** Unmatched: basic strategy guide, Basic strategy, UNMATCHED Advanced Fundamentals 3: Advantage and Draft

## Sidekicks

**In short:** Sidekicks act as supplementary units that provide extra attacks, board control, and tactical blocking options alongside your hero.

* Certain heroes are accompanied by sidekicks with their own health values and unique cards. Sidekicks can pin opponents, absorb damage, and control key spaces on the map.
* On smaller maps, you can use your sidekicks to physically block enemy heroes, forcing opponents to attack the sidekicks or burn movement boosts to bypass them.
* Managing your sidekicks properly prevents them from becoming liability targets while maximizing their ability to chip away at enemy health or secure map zones.

**Term:** Sidekick Control

**Sources:** Unmatched: basic strategy guide, How to Play Unmatched: A Comprehensive Guide, Basic strategy

## The clock

**In short:** Running out of cards triggers exhaustion, dealing direct damage every time a card should be drawn and turning the game into a race against the deck.

* Most decks have 30 cards and are never reshuffled (a few fighters have smaller decks or ways to recycle cards). Once your deck is empty, you are exhausted. When maneuvering or performing actions that require drawing from an empty deck, your hero takes 2 damage for each card you should have drawn.
* Playing for exhaustion involves passing with non-drawing schemes or managing your card usage so that your opponent runs out of cards and takes fatal exhaustion damage first.
* While rushing to kill your opponent quickly is one victory path, managing your deck count and surviving until the late game is a completely viable alternative win condition.

**Term:** Exhaustion Clock

**Sources:** How to Play Unmatched: A Comprehensive Guide, Basic strategy, Exhaustion tips

## Win conditions and play patterns

**In short:** Games are won either through aggressive pressure, explosive combos, high-value resource efficiency, or outlasting the opponent until exhaustion.

* Aggressive play patterns involve maintaining constant pressure, attacking nearly every turn, and forcing your opponent to burn through their defensive resources.
* Combo and burst play patterns focus on conservative chip damage while setting up a massive one-turn kill using a sequence of high-value cards and action gains.
* Value play patterns prioritize card efficiency, using superior deck quality and careful positioning to gradually dismantle the opponent's options over a longer game.

**Term:** Play Pattern

**Sources:** Basic strategy, Tips - How to play aggro?, UNMATCHED Advanced Fundamentals 2: Win Conditions and Play Patterns

## Reading the matchup and adapting

**In short:** Every hero pairing presents unique dynamics that require you to identify whether you should play aggressively or defensively based on the board state.

* You must determine who holds the natural initiative in a given matchup by comparing card draw, movement, and damage output. If your opponent has superior card draw and pressure, you may need to adopt a defensive posture.
* Adapting mid-game involves recognizing when your initial strategy is failing and pivoting—such as shifting from an aggressive approach to a conservative survival plan as your hand dwindles.
* Studying specific opponent card kits helps you anticipate deadly sequences, allowing you to hold your defensive cancels for their most threatening attacks.

**Term:** Matchup Adaptation

**Sources:** Unmatched: basic strategy guide, Basic strategy, UNMATCHED Advanced Fundamentals 2: Win Conditions and Play Patterns

---

## The draft

**In short:** Selecting fighters and maps in a structured draft format allows you to exploit terrain advantages and counter your opponent's lineup.

* Draft formats such as arsenal or conquest require players to pick or ban fighters and maps strategically. Map selection should match your fighter's optimal operating range, choosing small maps for aggressive melee fighters and large maps for ranged kiters.
* Split advantage separates map choice from starting position choice, letting you mitigate unfavorable terrain or force your opponent into an awkward starting setup.
* Avoid draft traps like picking multiple fighters who rely entirely on small maps or overcommitting to counter-picks at the expense of your overall lineup cohesion.

**Term:** Strategic Draft

**Sources:** UNMATCHED Advanced Fundamentals 3: Advantage and Draft

## Mentality and getting better

**In short:** Improving at Unmatched requires maintaining a growth mindset, analyzing your mistakes objectively, and engaging constructively with the community.

* Focus on controllable factors like key tactical interactions, positioning mistakes, and matchup nuances rather than blaming bad luck or poor deck order.
* Treat losses as learning opportunities by evaluating what went wrong during critical turns and adjusting your decision-making for future matches.
* Maintain good sportsmanship and build relationships with training partners or local leagues to accelerate your understanding of competitive fundamentals.

**Term:** Competitive Mentality

**Sources:** UNMATCHED Advanced Fundamentals 4: Mentality and Improving

## Glossary

* **Card Advantage:** The tactical benefit of holding more cards in hand than your opponent to ensure versatile attack and defense options.
* **Tempo:** The rhythm of the game determined by the shifting control of initiative between players.
* **Combat Exchange:** The calculated sequence of attacking and defending that weighs immediate damage against future vulnerability.
* **Card Counting:** Tracking public discard piles and remaining deck contents to anticipate enemy plays and cancels.
* **Boosting:** Discarding a card for its boost value, to move further or, where a card allows it, to strengthen an attack.
* **Map Positioning:** Managing board zones, distances, and terrain features to optimize melee or ranged effectiveness.
* **Sidekick Control:** Utilizing supplementary fighter units to block paths, absorb damage, and secure board presence.
* **Exhaustion Clock:** The late-game timer where drawing from an empty deck deals direct damage to your hero.
* **Play Pattern:** The overarching strategic approach—such as aggro, combo, value, or fatigue—used to secure victory.
* **Matchup Adaptation:** Evaluating hero dynamics mid-game to shift between offensive pressure and defensive survival.
* **Strategic Draft:** Selecting complementary fighters and maps to maximize terrain advantage and counter opponents.
* **Competitive Mentality:** A focused, objective approach to learning from mistakes and maintaining positive community engagement.
