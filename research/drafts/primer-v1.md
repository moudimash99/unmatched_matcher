# Unmatched strategy primer

Unmatched is primarily a game of managing the cards in your hand, followed by your deck, with movement and positioning serving to execute your plans.

## Card Advantage and Hand Size
- **In short:** Maintaining a larger hand size than your opponent gives you more offensive and defensive options to control the pace of the game.
- Having more cards in hand means your options are more versatile and you can handle incoming attacks or push pressure effectively. 
- A full hand of seven cards is rare and usually indicates an imbalance of either attacks or defenses; if you hold too many cards of one type, you must go on the offensive to clear your hand.
- Letting your hand dwindle leaves you vulnerable, forcing you to maneuver repeatedly to draw cards, which surrenders momentum and risks drawing bad cards.
- **Term:** hand advantage

## Tempo and Initiative
- **In short:** Initiative dictates who must approach and act as the aggressor, while tempo is the rhythm and exchange of that initiative.
- The player holding the initiative forces the pace, dictates engagement frequency, and applies pressure, but they also have fewer tactical options and must constantly approach.
- You can punish an opponent's initiative by counter-pressuring them with double attacks or high-value strikes, boosting away to force them to spend resources chasing you, or riding the wave by letting them gas themselves out.
- You can pass initiative back to your opponent by double maneuvering or utilizing specific passes to make them take the active role.
- **Term:** tempo

## Trading Damage and Attacking vs Defending
- **In short:** Combat decisions require weighing the immediate damage dealt against the cost of burning your finite defensive resources.
- Attack values average around four while defenses average around three, meaning standard attacks often deal small, un-guaranteed damage unless enhanced by card effects or boosts.
- Attacking twice in a turn forces the opponent to burn through their hand quickly, but it risks leaving you vulnerable to cancels if you overextend.
- Balancing when to attack and when to defend prevents you from running out of crucial defensive cards or failing to press your opponent's low health pool.
- **Term:** trading damage

## Counting Cards (Feints and Other Answers)
- **In short:** Tracking public information like discards and anticipating key cards prevents you from walking into devastating cancels and trap effects.
- Feints and cancel effects are critical defensive tools that strip away your opponent's card effects and disrupt their big offensive combos.
- Keeping an eye on the opponent's discard pile allows you to calculate whether critical cards like Feints have already been played, making your big attacks safer to commit.
- Unpredictability in how you deploy your attacks and defenses prevents your opponent from easily reading your plays or denying your key effects.
- **Term:** card counting

## Boosting
- **In short:** Boosting lets you spend cards from your hand to increase movement or enhance card effects, trading long-term hand size for immediate tactical positioning.
- Boosting allows slower fighters to close gaps or aggressive fighters to relentlessly pursue opponents across the map.
- Because cards are a finite resource, overusing boosts will rapidly drain your hand and accelerate your progress toward exhaustion.
- **Term:** boosting

## Positioning, Range and Zones
- **In short:** Utilizing the board's colored zones, choke points, and distances allows you to maximize your fighter's range advantages and control engagement.
- Ranged fighters thrive by keeping distance and attacking across spaces within the same zone, while melee fighters must close adjacent spaces to engage.
- Smaller maps favor melee brawlers and fighters who want to block paths with sidekicks, whereas larger maps benefit mobile fighters and ranged characters who want space to kite.
- Proper positioning keeps you safe from counterattacks, prevents you from getting pinned in corners, and forces your opponent to waste movement or boosts.
- **Term:** positioning

## Sidekicks
- **In short:** Sidekicks act as supplementary units that provide board control, body blocking, and extra offensive or defensive utility.
- Sidekicks can be positioned to block narrow corridors or choke points, forcing opponents to waste movement or burn boosts to reach your hero.
- Managing your sidekicks involves balancing their survival with their ability to contribute damage or trigger specific card effects, ensuring they do not become easy targets that bleed your health.
- **Term:** sidekicks

## The Clock (Exhaustion and Racing)
- **In short:** When your deck runs out, drawing cards deals direct damage to your hero, turning the game into a race against exhaustion.
- Exhaustion damage deals two damage for every card you are forced to draw from an empty deck, making card preservation a critical late-game survival mechanic.
- Passes, such as schemes or attacks that do not draw extra cards, help you stay out of exhaustion longer while pressuring an opponent who is forced to keep cycling their deck.
- **Term:** exhaustion

## Win Conditions and Play Patterns
- **In short:** Victories are achieved through distinct play patterns like aggressive pressure, combo bursts, value accumulation, or forcing exhaustion.
- Aggro playstyles focus on relentless attacks and denying the opponent breathing room, whereas value playstyles rely on card quality, efficiency, and outlasting opponents.
- Recognizing your win condition relative to your opponent's strategy dictates whether you should push the offensive or slow the game down for the late game.
- **Term:** play patterns

## Reading the Matchup and Adapting
- **In short:** Success in Unmatched depends on understanding both fighters' strengths and adapting your strategy to counter the opponent's specific toolset.
- Evaluating matchup dynamics helps you determine whether you should take the aggressor role or play defensively from turn one.
- Adapting to the board state means recognizing when your initial game plan is failing and pivoting to alternative tactics, such as transitioning from aggression into a late-game exhaustion strategy.
- **Term:** matchup adaptation

## Mentality and Getting Better
- **In short:** Maintaining a calm, growth-oriented mindset helps you learn from mistakes, handle bad matchups, and improve consistently over time.
- Focusing on controllable factors like tactical execution and key interactions rather than bad card draw or luck keeps your mental game sharp.
- Engaging with the community, analyzing your misplays, and keeping your emotions neutral during a match directly translates to better decision-making and long-term improvement.
- **Term:** mentality

---

## Glossary
- **Hand advantage:** Having more cards in hand than your opponent to ensure tactical flexibility.
- **Tempo:** The rhythm, speed, and shifting of initiative throughout the match.
- **Trading damage:** Weighing the value of damage dealt against the defensive resources spent.
- **Card counting:** Keeping track of discards and public information to anticipate opponent answers.
- **Boosting:** Spending extra cards from your hand to increase movement or enhance effects.
- **Positioning:** Controlling spaces on the board relative to zones and opposing fighters.
- **Sidekicks:** Supplementary units that assist the hero with board control and combat.
- **Exhaustion:** The end-game state where drawing from an empty deck inflicts direct damage.
- **Play patterns:** The overarching strategic approaches used to secure a win.
- **Matchup adaptation:** Adjusting your strategy dynamically based on the specific fighters and board state.
- **Mentality:** The psychological approach of staying objective, learning from mistakes, and maintaining sportsmanship.
