## Daredevil

**In short:** A tough melee fighter with lots of health and a tiny 22-card deck, who wants a long game where he heals, recycles his best cards and never runs out.

### Plan A: Outlast them
**Goal:** Win the long game. While most fighters slowly run out of cards, Daredevil keeps cycling his own back in.
**How to do it:** Devil of Hell's Kitchen shuffles itself and the top four cards of your discard pile back into your deck, so it can be replayed again and again. Before you play it, make sure the last four cards you discarded include Take A Knee, Breather, Through Adversity or Feint. That way your healing and recovery cards come back each cycle. Defend with Take A Knee to heal 2 health most of the time, and use Breather to pull a key card back from your discard pile.
**Watch out for:** A Feint on Devil of Hell's Kitchen cancels the reshuffle. When your deck is nearly empty, keep a second copy or a Breather in hand as a backup.

### Plan B: Play without fear
**Goal:** Get damage through even though most of his cards are only worth 2 or 3.
**How to do it:** With 2 or fewer cards in hand, his ability lets you blind boost your attack or defense, so a small hand is a strength, not a problem. Man Without Fear has its own blind boost on top, so it can be boosted twice. When your deck is empty, Devil of Hell's Kitchen becomes an 8-value attack.
**Watch out for:** A blind boost can throw away the cards you need, especially Devil of Hell's Kitchen and Breather. Boost when the combat calls for it, not just because you can.

### Plan C: Get in their face
**Goal:** Close the distance on ranged fighters and on fighters who need time to set up.
**How to do it:** Through Adversity moves him up to 4 spaces through enemy fighters and deals 1 damage to each one he passes. Grappling Hook gives him 2 more spaces. Against fighters who need setup, keep attacking so they have to spend turns drawing defenses instead of building their combo.
**Watch out for:** Ranged fighters with sidekicks can keep hitting him while he chases.

### How they lose
- His recycling is shut down. Opponents with lots of Feints, or effects that make him discard cards, cancel his healing and the Devil of Hell's Kitchen reshuffle. A 22-card deck runs out fast without it.
- He is kept at range by fighters who hit from afar and screen with sidekicks, such as Medusa or Robin Hood.
- He blind boosts carelessly and discards his own key cards.

### Key cards
- **Devil of Hell's Kitchen**: his most important card. It keeps him from ever running out of cards. Save it for when your deck is empty, unless your opponent expects that; then playing it early can catch them off guard.
- **Breather**: brings a discarded attack, defense or versatile card back to your hand. Use it first on Devil of Hell's Kitchen.
- **Take A Knee**: a 3-value versatile card that heals. It's best used on defense.
- **Son Of A Boxer**: a 3-value defense. If you lose the combat, it deals 2 damage to an adjacent fighter, which makes it good insurance against melee fighters.
- **Through Adversity**: his way through screens of sidekicks.

### Tips
- Don't worry about running low on cards. A hand of 2 or fewer is what turns on his blind boost.
- Almost all his defenses are worth 3, so you can defend reliably even from a small hand.
- Plan the four cards that Devil of Hell's Kitchen will shuffle back before you play it.
- When your deck is about to run out, hold two copies of Devil of Hell's Kitchen, or one copy and a Breather.
- Against fighters who build up combos, keep a bigger hand so they can't hit you while you have no defense.
