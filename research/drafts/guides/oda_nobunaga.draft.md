## Oda Nobunaga

**In short:** A highly aggressive, positioning-heavy melee fighter. He relies on his two Honor Guards to set up flanking traps and attack with his zone buff, effectively turning his sidekicks into his primary win condition.

### Plan A: Trap and crush

**Goal:** Pin the opponent with flanking positioning and hit them with enhanced Honor Guard attacks.
**How to do it:** An opposing fighter is flanked when they are adjacent to two or more of your fighters. Move the Honor Guards into Oda's zone so they receive his +1 value buff, then surround the enemy. Once the target is flanked, your Honor Guard attacks are devastating. Sun and Moon ignores the opponent's defense value, making it an unblockable 3 damage (with the buff) unless they cancel it. Lightning and Thunder becomes a 5-value attack that cancels their effects and lets you move away after combat. This creates a trap: opponents want to Feint Sun and Moon, but will take heavy damage if they guess wrong and you play Lightning and Thunder instead. Fire and Flames is an any-fighter attack that jumps to a 5 value, or 6 if played by a buffed Honor Guard.
**Watch out for:** You only have three fighters on the board. If even one Honor Guard dies, flanking becomes incredibly difficult. If both die, you can no longer flank at all, leaving half your deck as blank or useless cards.

### Plan B: Draw heavily to maintain the assault

**Goal:** Dig for your flanking tools and play your best cards before the Honor Guards are killed.
**How to do it:** Your Honor Guards have low printed defenses, so they will rarely survive a long match. Play aggressively and draw cards to maintain constant pressure. Student of War is a 5-value attack that draws cards equal to the damage dealt. Reinforce gives you flexible scheme options, but choosing to draw two cards and gain an action is the best way to starve the opponent's hand and keep attacking. Use Battle Maneuvers defensively: it blocks for 2 (or 3 for a buffed Honor Guard), draws a card, and lets you move each of your fighters up to 2 spaces. This allows you to position a perfect flank during their turn, leaving all your actions free to strike on yours.
**Watch out for:** Be careful with Student of War. If you attack a 1-health sidekick who decides not to defend, you will deal 5 damage and draw five cards, heavily accelerating your exhaustion clock.

### Plan C: Swap to spread the damage

**Goal:** Keep both Honor Guards alive for as long as possible to maintain your flanking threat.
**How to do it:** Since your Honor Guards share the damage burden, keeping both on the board is vital. Spring the Trap is a 2-value defense that lets the defending fighter swap spaces with an adjacent friendly fighter, who then takes the attack instead. Use this to shift an attack from a wounded Honor Guard to a healthy one, or to Oda himself. After combat, it also deals 1 damage to the attacker. Momentous Shift is your only way for an Honor Guard to block for a printed value of 3, which becomes a 4 with the buff.
**Watch out for:** The sources disagree on Patience and Strategy. One praises it as a way to heal the Honor Guards and bait out strong blocks. Another argues it rarely works on defense because opponents will simply maneuver to avoid the flank before attacking, and playing it on attack wastes a precious action you need for dealing real damage.

### How they lose

​- The opponent focuses their attacks exclusively on the Honor Guards. Once they drop, Oda loses his buff, his flanking tools, and almost all his offensive pressure.
​- Damage effects that trigger outside of combat bypass their weak defenses and quickly kill the Honor Guards.
​- Pragmatism is his only defensive cancel, but it can only be played by Oda. The Honor Guards have no way to cancel effects on defense.
​- The opponent constantly maneuvers away. Oda has only five cards with a boost value of 3 (Demon King of the Sixth Heaven and Fire and Flames), making it very hard for him to catch fast ranged fighters.

### Key cards

​- **Student of War**: his biggest attack. Best used against the enemy hero to draw cards and keep your hand full.
​- **Lightning and Thunder**: a high-value attack that cancels effects on a flank and then lets your Honor Guard step out of danger.
​- **Sun and Moon**: a cheap attack that guarantees damage against anything but a cancel when the opponent is flanked.
​- **Battle Maneuvers**: a versatile card that provides free movement to set up flanks without spending actions.
​- **Reinforce**: a scheme that keeps your momentum going with card draw and an extra action.

### Tips

​- Treat your Honor Guards as your main damage source, not just extra bodies.
​- Keep your 3-boost cards as movement fuel against opponents who run away. The sources disagree heavily on Demon King of the Sixth Heaven: one calls it reliable area damage, while another insists you should never play it for its effect and only keep it to boost.
​- Always check that an Honor Guard is in Oda's zone before you play their attack or defense card to ensure they receive the +1 buff.
​- If you use Spring the Trap to swap fighters, make sure the new defender hasn't accidentally left Oda's zone and lost their defense buff.
