## Doctor Strange

**In short:** A ranged fighter who outlasts opponents by taking damage to recycle his best cards, then healing back up. He passes turns efficiently to force exhaustion, and shuts down melee attackers with adjacency tricks.

### Plan A: Outlast them with endless cards and healing

**Goal:** Recycle key cards to maintain card advantage and wear down the opponent's health.
**How to do it:** His special ability, Dark Pact, lets him take 1 damage after combat to place the card he just played on the bottom of his deck and draw a new one. This keeps his hand full and his deck large. The Rings of Raggadorr (3 copies, value 4) heals him and adjacent friendly fighters for the damage dealt. If you hit an undefended 1-health sidekick or an opponent with no blocks left, you deal 4 damage and heal 4. Paying 1 health to recycle it nets you 3 health and keeps the threat alive. Because recurred cards go to the bottom of your deck, use The Winds of Watoomb (3 copies) to teleport fighters to starting spaces and shuffle the decks, bringing your recycled cards back into the mix.
**Watch out for:** Dark Pact only triggers if Doctor Strange himself plays the card. Schemes are not combat cards, and Wong cannot use the ability. Playing defenses for Wong wastes cards that Strange could have recycled.

### Plan B: Stall the exhaustion clock

**Goal:** Burn turns without shrinking your deck, forcing the opponent to run out of cards first.
**How to do it:** By passing turns safely, you force the opponent to take the initiative and spend cards maneuvering toward you. When they run out of cards, the exhaustion clock deals damage every time they try to draw. Eye of Agamotto (2 copies) is a scheme that shuffles your hand into your deck, draws five cards, and gives an action. You can maneuver, play this to replace your hand, and maneuver again. Your turn ends, but your deck is barely smaller. No Really, I'm a Doctor (2 copies) works similarly: reveal a card from your hand, put it on top of your deck, and heal by its boost value. Reveal Bolts of Balthakk (4 copies, boost 3), heal for 3, and draw it on your next maneuver. Stay back and watch them exhaust.
**Watch out for:** Relentless pressure. If an aggressive opponent attacks every turn, you will have to use your cards to defend rather than relying on schemes to pass time.

### Plan C: Punish adjacent attackers

**Goal:** Turn his ranged status into an advantage when fighters close in.
**How to do it:** Melee fighters who get next to him face Cloak of Levitation (2 copies, value 2). If he is adjacent to the attacker, it ignores the value of their card entirely, effectively blocking any printed attack value. Master of Kamar-Taj (3 copies) becomes a 4-value block when Strange and Wong are adjacent. Use sidekick control to your advantage: Wong can stand in narrow corridors to body-block, soaking hits while Strange attacks from safety. If you are cornered, The Winds of Watoomb can reset the board's positioning.
**Watch out for:** Cloak of Levitation ignores values, but can be canceled by an offensive Feint (like She-Hulk's), leaving you taking the full hit. Master of Kamar-Taj becomes a weak 2-value card once Wong is defeated.

### How they lose

- Aggressive fighters with extra actions and relentless pressure (T-Rex, Bruce Lee) force him to burn defenses faster than he can heal.
- Big printed attacks get through his standard 2-value blocks if he gets caught without Cloak of Levitation or Wong.
- Using his ability too often without landing a successful heal leaves his health dangerously low.
- He relies heavily on his deck quality. If he blind boosts away his best cards, they are lost forever, as discarded cards cannot be retrieved with his ability.

### Key cards

- **The Rings of Raggadorr**: his survival engine. Recur it often and aim for unblocked targets or low-health sidekicks for a massive health swing.
- **Cloak of Levitation**: his absolute best defense against melee fighters. Take the damage to put it back in your deck whenever you use it.
- **Eye of Agamotto**: a scheme that resets your hand, shuffles recurred cards away from the bottom of your deck, and burns an action safely.
- **The Mists of Munnopor**: a 2-value defense that discards the opponent's card and forces them to play the next attack or versatile they reveal. The sources disagree on it: some use it to cancel heavy hitters like Dracula's Beastform, while others avoid it because pulling a random card can accidentally turn a weak attack into a deadly one.
- **Seven Suns of Cinnibus**: a 3-value versatile that can be blind boosted up to two times. If the total value goes over 7, it becomes zero, making a double blind boost extremely risky.
- **Bolts of Balthakk**: a 2-value attack that grants an action if you win. It sets up devastating combo turns, but blind boosting it might cost you a key card.

### Tips

- Do not blind boost unless you are pushing for a quick kill. Strange needs to keep his best cards in rotation, and a card discarded for its boost cannot be recovered.
- Before playing The Winds of Watoomb, use Dark Pact on a few combats first so those recycled cards get shuffled into the deck rather than sitting at the bottom.
- The sources disagree on how to handle Wong: some keep him alive to use as a meat shield and heal him, while others let him die quickly to avoid spending un-recyclable cards on his defense.
- Keep Strange and Wong adjacent whenever possible to maximize Master of Kamar-Taj and heal both with The Rings of Raggadorr.
- Against fighters with offensive cancels (like She-Hulk), bait out their Feints with regular blocks before risking your Cloak of Levitation.
- Use Wong's Steadfast Disciple (3 copies) to chip away at fighters safely, as it deals automatic damage after combat if he remains adjacent.
