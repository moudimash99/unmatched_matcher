## Chupacabra

**In short:** An aggressive solo melee fighter who draws cards by attacking. He strips the opponent's hand with discard effects, chains multiple attacks together, and unlocks his strongest card values only after his health drops to 7 or below.

### Plan A: Relentless pressure and double attacks

**Goal:** Run the opponent out of defensive cards through constant attacks, then land a heavy finisher.
**How to do it:** His special ability, The Hunger, lets you draw a card after you attack. This fuels constant aggression because every attack replaces itself. You want to hit the opponent twice in one turn. Set this up with Traveler of the night. It moves you up to 4 spaces without maneuvering and refunds its action, leaving you with two actions to attack. Start with Ambush, which forces a discard even if they defend, or use a standard attack to win the combat. For your second action, play Blood in the air. If you already won a combat this turn, you may boost Blood in the air. With a hand full of 3-boost cards, this easily hits for 7.
**Watch out for:** Drawing a card on every attack burns through your deck quickly. Sources disagree on how to manage this: some say you should attack and draw as much as possible to overwhelm the opponent, while others suggest skipping the optional draw once you have a healthy hand size so you can play for exhaustion.

### Plan B: The low-health sweet spot

**Goal:** Activate his most powerful effects by dropping to half health, then stay alive with minor healing.
**How to do it:** Two of his best cards require him to be at 7 health or less. Wounded beast becomes a 5-value versatile, making it his hardest unboosted hit or his strongest block. Feeding refunds an action and heals him for 1. To reach this threshold on your own terms, use Unsettle. It forces a discard, and you can choose to deal 2 damage to yourself to make them discard a second card. Once you drop to 7, you become incredibly dangerous. You can manage this sweet spot with his healing cards. Ravenous lunge and Tooth and tail give you small bursts of health to keep you from dying while ideally keeping you under the threshold.
**Watch out for:** Dropping to 7 health too early leaves you vulnerable to one-turn kills. His printed defensive values are low, mostly topping out at 3, so fighters with heavy burst damage or big combos can easily clear your remaining 7 health before you secure the win.

### Plan C: Hand destruction and mind games

**Goal:** Empty their hand so they are forced to take unblocked damage.
**How to do it:** Unsettle and Ambush both force random discards, ripping defensive options away from the opponent. Once their hand is low, you create a trap with The more they struggle. It is a 0-value attack, but its value becomes twice the printed value of the opponent's defense card. If they fear a big attack and play a 4-value defense, you hit them for 8. If they guess you are playing The more they struggle and choose to take it undefended, the attack deals zero damage. However, taking an attack undefended means they lose the combat, which perfectly sets up Blood in the air for your next action.
**Watch out for:** The more they struggle does not work against effects that prevent values from changing (like Jill Trent) or abilities that force you to declare your attack value (like Spider-Man). It is also weak against defensive cards that get their power from effects rather than printed values, like Ghost Rider's Penance Stare.

### How they lose

**Goal:**

- He exhausts himself. His ability provides massive card draw, but he has no way to recycle cards back into his deck.
- Fighters with heavy attacks punch right through his 3-value defenses.
- He gets surrounded by sidekicks. As a solo melee fighter, he struggles against multiple bodies blocking his path to the hero.
- He gets caught at 7 health. A skilled opponent will save their biggest attacks until he drops his health to activate his abilities, killing him instantly.

### Key cards

- **Blood in the air**: His finisher. A 4-value attack that you can boost if you already won a combat this turn.
- **The more they struggle**: A 0-value attack that punishes high-value blocks by doubling their printed number.
- **Unsettle**: A scheme that forces a discard, or two discards if you deal 2 damage to Chupacabra. Essential for pushing his health down to 7.
- **Feeding**: A 4-value attack. If you are at 7 health or less, it refunds an action and heals 1. His only action-gain attack.
- **Wounded beast**: A 3-value versatile that jumps to 5 if he is at 7 health or less. Sources recommend using this on offense rather than saving it to block.
- **Natural toughness**: A 3-value defense that returns to your hand if you lose the combat. It provides a reusable block to help him survive while taking chip damage to reach his low-health threshold.

### Tips

- Players debate the optimal hand size for him. One source recommends keeping exactly 6 cards to avoid discarding. Another argues that keeping 5 cards is better, because it allows you to double maneuver on your next turn without going over the 7-card limit.
- Don't waste Tooth and tail on defense. Since you want to hit exactly two adjacent fighters to maximize the healing, play it as an attack where you can control your positioning first.
- Be careful when blocking with Natural toughness. Opponents who know the deck will bait it out with low-value attacks like Regroup, hoping you win the combat so the card goes to your discard pile instead of returning to your hand.
- If you have two copies of Traveler of the night in hand, you can use one to maneuver and the second as a 3-value boost for Blood in the air.
- Pay close attention to your healing. If you are at 7 health and win a combat with Ravenous lunge, the 2 points of healing will push you to 9, turning off Feeding and Wounded beast until you take damage again.
