## Geralt of Rivia

**In short:** A flexible melee fighter who chooses his key equipment before the game begins. He hits hard and disrupts his opponent's hand, but relies on his fragile sidekick and struggles with mobility.

### Plan A: Draft the perfect gear

**Goal:** Select the right Sword, Armor, and Potion to counter the opponent's game plan.
**How to do it:** Before the deck is shuffled, you add two copies each of an attack, a defense, and a scheme gear card.
​- **Sword**: Sword of silver (value 4) ignores defenses with a printed value of 4 or higher, making it devastating against fighters who rely on big blocks like Bigfoot. Sword of steel (value 4) grants extra actions for a burst turn, especially against high-health enemies.
​- **Armor**: Wolf Medallion (value 3) cancels effects and ignores the value of ranged attacks. Armor of the Forgotten Wolf (value 2) ends the turn and gains value for every adjacent fighter, punishing swarm characters like the Raptors or anyone relying on hit-and-run action gain.
​- **Potion**: Both potions stay on the board as an ongoing effect until you finish a turn without attacking, which forces you to play aggressively. Blizzard prevents opponents from gaining actions, while Tawny Owl draws two cards, grants an action, and prevents discard effects.

Use Witcher Senses to find your gear early.
**Watch out for:** The sources disagree on the potions. One user argues Tawny Owl is always better because you can just use its extra action to boost a maneuver and replicate Blizzard's movement, while others value Blizzard's ability to lock down enemy action loops.

### Plan B: Mix up your heavy hits

**Goal:** Break through defenses by keeping the opponent guessing.
**How to do it:** Geralt has multiple ways to bypass a standard block. If they play a strong defense, Sword of silver ignores its value. If they play a weak defense, Rend lets you boost your attack value after seeing their card; feed it your 4-boost cards like Damn, you're ugly to hit for 7. If you expect a cancel, Igni has a value of 0 but guarantees 2 damage after combat. Use Dandelion's Plot twist to look at their hand first, discard their best defense, and pick the perfect attack for whatever they have left.
**Watch out for:** Igni deals 1 damage to *each other* adjacent fighter. This hits Dandelion too, so make sure your sidekick steps back before you cast it.

### Plan C: Cash in Dandelion early

**Goal:** Get value from your sidekick before he is eliminated.
**How to do it:** Geralt's deck contains no ANY cards. Dandelion can only play his own six specific cards: three copies of Plot twist (to strip their hand) and three of Annoying tune (to push them away). Use them as quickly as possible to disrupt the opponent and draw cards.
**Watch out for:** Dandelion only has 5 health and dies fast. Once he is gone, his remaining cards become dead draws that can only be used for boosting.

### How they lose

​- He bleeds value through movement. With a base move of 2 and no passive mobility, he has to burn cards to boost toward ranged kiters, leaving him with a small hand.
​- He cannibalizes his own resources. His strongest cards are versatiles. If an aggressive opponent forces him to play Damn, you're ugly or Disciplined Duelist on defense, he has nothing left to threaten them with on his turn.
​- He has a high starting health of 16, but zero healing in his deck. Every mistake is permanent.
​- His sidekick is easily picked off, which deletes his only hand-disruption tool.

### Key cards

​- **Witcher Senses**: a scheme that pulls a gear card from your deck or discard pile. If you search your deck, Witcher Senses shuffles back in, allowing you to play it again later to recycle gear from the discard pile.
​- **Damn, you're ugly**: a 5-value versatile. It hits extremely hard on its own and provides a massive 4-value boost for Rend or maneuvers.
​- **Disciplined Duelist**: a 4-value versatile that triggers different effects based on the opponent's printed value, letting you draw a card, deal damage, or force a discard.
​- **Plot twist**: a 2-value versatile for Dandelion. Hand knowledge is incredibly powerful for setting up Geralt's tailored attacks.
​- **Rend**: a 3-value versatile that you may boost during combat.

### Tips

​- Armor of the Forgotten Wolf gains +1 for *each* adjacent fighter. Standing next to Dandelion adds to its defensive value.
​- Use Yrden to freeze an opponent in their space. This either lets Geralt step away safely to avoid a second attack, or locks down a ranged fighter so they can't run on their next turn.
​- If you play Rend on defense, remember that the defender's effects resolve first. You must decide whether to boost before the attacker decides to use any boost effects of their own.
