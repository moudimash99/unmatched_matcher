## Robin Hood

**In short:** A fast, aggressive ranged fighter who avoids damage through constant movement rather than blocking. He surrounds himself with sidekicks to physically block the enemy and plays mind games with unblockable attacks, while safely shooting from a distance.

### Plan A: Hit and run

**Goal:** Attack from safety and force the opponent to spend actions catching you.
**How to do it:** His special ability lets the attacking fighter move up to 2 spaces after an attack. Because Robin Hood is ranged, he can attack from the edge of a zone and immediately retreat out of reach. A melee opponent will have to spend their first action maneuvering just to get close, making it nearly impossible for them to attack him twice in one turn. This constant kiting prevents incoming attacks entirely, compensating for his fragile defenses. The ability works for his sidekicks, too: an Outlaw can run in, strike, and step back.
**Watch out for:** His deck contains massive card draw through effects like Disarming Shot and Piercing Shot. Drawing so many cards puts him on a fast exhaustion clock. You cannot just run away and delay the game; you must maintain aggressive pressure and finish the fight before your deck runs out.

### Plan B: The Highway Robbery trap

**Goal:** Punish the opponent whether they defend your sidekicks or not.
**How to do it:** Highway Robbery (4 copies, Outlaw only) cancels all card effects and ignores the defense value. When an Outlaw attacks, the opponent faces a dilemma: play a Feint to cancel the Robbery, or play a normal defense, see it ignored, and take 2 unpreventable damage anyway. Once opponents start taking the damage undefended to save their Feints, punish them by attacking with Regroup to safely win the combat and draw two cards. You can also slip in Ambush (value 2), forcing a random discard and adding the discarded card's boost to your attack for heavy, undefended damage.
**Watch out for:** Outlaws are melee, so they have to get close to set this up. Because opponents hoard their Feints for the Outlaws, Robin Hood's own attacks are more likely to resolve without being canceled.

### Plan C: The Outlaw barricade

**Goal:** Protect Robin's small health pool.
**How to do it:** Robin Hood has only 13 health, and his highest printed block is a 3. To survive, position your four Outlaws around him to physically block paths and doorways. Let the Outlaws take the hits meant for him. Defenders of Sherwood is a 3-value defense that revives a defeated Outlaw to any space in Robin's zone, effectively turning your four sidekicks into six or more over the course of the game. Use Wily Fighting defensively: it deals 1 damage to adjacent opposing fighters after combat. Because defender effects resolve first, the damage triggers before the attacker can use any movement effects to escape.
**Watch out for:** Any attack of 4 or higher guarantees a dead Outlaw. Sometimes it is better to take the hit completely undefended and save your limited block cards for Robin himself.

### How they lose

​- Big printed attacks (like Bigfoot's 6s) bleed right through his 3-value defenses, melting his low health.
​- Area-of-effect damage clears out his Outlaw barricades quickly, leaving him exposed.
​- He draws so many cards that he often reaches exhaustion before the opponent, losing the game to his own deck.
​- Opponents with movement after combat (like Bruce Lee) or effects that move him out of position can keep pace with his retreats and trap him.

### Key cards

​- **Highway Robbery**: The core of the Outlaw mind game. It deals 2 damage and wastes the opponent's defense card unless they play a cancel.
​- **A Hunter's Eye**: A 5-value attack with no text. It hits hard, cannot be canceled, and heavily punishes opponents who guess wrong and waste a Feint on it.
​- **Disarming Shot**: A 4-value attack that draws cards equal to the damage dealt. If played against an undefended 1-health sidekick, it deals 4 damage and draws 4 cards.
​- **Defenders of Sherwood**: His most important defense card. Save it strictly for when you have a defeated Outlaw in the discard pile.
​- **Steal From the Rich**: A scheme that draws a card and makes the opponent choose between discarding or letting you draw again. The sources disagree on its value: one calls it a tricky tactical choice that forces mistakes, while others consider it a weak action better spent as a 3-value boost.

### Tips

​- Use his ability on the Outlaws too. They can step up, attack, and retreat out of range just like Robin to avoid counterattacks.
​- Save your Feints for Robin. Your Outlaws will usually die to a determined attack anyway, so don't waste a cancel trying to save them.
​- A Hunter's Eye has a high boost value of 4. Don't hold it in your hand against fighters who use Ambush, or they will randomly discard it and add 4 to their own attack.
​- Spread your Outlaws out against opponents with area damage to prevent losing multiple sidekicks to a single card.
​- If an Outlaw attacks with Snark or Wily Fighting and is defeated, the fighter is removed from the board and is no longer adjacent, so the after-combat effect will not trigger.
