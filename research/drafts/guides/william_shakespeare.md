## William Shakespeare

**In short:** A complex, aggressive melee fighter who strings cards together to form 10-syllable lines. He relies on powerful unblockable effects and extra actions, but with low health and absolutely no scheme or defense cards, he heavily depends on his three Actors to absorb damage.

### Plan A: Threaten extra actions

**Goal:** Complete a line with My Kingdom For a Horse to take back-to-back turns.
**How to do it:** His special ability, Iambic Pentameter, adds every card you play in combat to your line. When the line hits exactly 10 syllables, the last card's completion effect triggers. *My Kingdom For a Horse* (3 copies, value 5) is a 6-syllable attack that grants 2 extra actions on completion. To set this up, play a 4-syllable card on your first action, putting you at exactly 4 syllables. Your best setup is *All Are Punished* (2 copies, value 4). On your second action, attack with *My Kingdom For a Horse*. Not only is it a heavy hit, but you immediately gain 2 actions to retreat, maneuver, or press your advantage.
**Watch out for:** Because discards outside of combat do not add to your line, you cannot adjust your syllable count by boosting. Opponents can track your syllables and will anticipate the 6-syllable drop. Also, *All Are Punished* deals 1 damage to *each* other fighter in the zone, meaning you will easily kill your own 1-health Actors if they are standing too close.

### Plan B: The Horror finisher

**Goal:** Deal guaranteed damage that ignores blocks and Feints.
**How to do it:** *Horror* (5 copies, value 4) is a 2-syllable attack whose value increases for each other *Horror* in your line. Its real threat is the completion effect, which deals 1 damage to the opposing fighter for each other card in your line. Because it is a 2-syllable card, it is easy to fit at the end of an 8-syllable sequence. Do not wait for a perfect line of five *Horrors*. Playing it as the third or fourth card in a mixed line still yields guaranteed unblockable damage, turning a standard attack into a massive hit. Since completion effects trigger after combat and resolve directly from the special ability, a Feint does not cancel this damage.
**Watch out for:** Pushing for maximum value by playing four *Horrors* in a row is too risky and forces you to miss out on other completion effects.

### Plan C: Survive and sustain

**Goal:** Keep a fragile hero alive and drawing cards.
**How to do it:** Shakespeare lacks any dedicated defense cards and has very few blocks above a 3. He needs his sidekicks to take hits. Defend with the Actor-exclusive *Such Sweet Sorrow* (2 copies, value 4) to force opponents to waste big attacks on a minion, then use its effect to move up to 2 spaces away and dodge follow-up strikes. If the Actors die, *Once More Unto The Breach* (2 copies, value 3) returns a defeated Actor to his zone, and revives all of them if it completes a line. For Shakespeare himself, *Alas* (2 copies, value 2) is critical. It heals 1 if you lose the combat, but completing a line with it draws 2 cards and heals 2. Since he lacks any scheme cards to pass his turn with, completing *Alas* is his primary engine for health and drawing.
**Watch out for:** Because he relies entirely on playing combat cards to build his line and draw new cards, Shakespeare frequently has to put his fragile 13-health body in harm's way just to keep his deck moving.

### How they lose

- Big printed attacks easily break through his 2- and 3-value defenses. Once his Actors fall, Shakespeare drops fast.
- Opponents manipulate his line. They attack him when he is at 4 syllables to force him to play a block, ruining his setup for *My Kingdom For a Horse*, or they refuse to attack him when he needs to efficiently build his syllables.
- He gets starved of cards. *Alas* and *Revise* are his only reliable draw effects, and he has no non-combat cards to stall the tempo.
- Fighters who can ignore body blocks (like Bullseye) or fighters with constant auto-damage run him out of health before his combos assemble.

### Key cards

- **My Kingdom For a Horse**: The core of his aggressive pressure, offering back-to-back turns.
- **Horror**: His best tool for pushing unblockable damage to close out a game.
- **Alas**: His most crucial card for sustaining health and card draw.
- **Deceive** (3 copies, value 2): A versatile that cancels effects. On completion, it returns to your hand, giving you a reusable Feint.
- **Once More Unto The Breach**: Try to use it on defense to revive all the Actors when the line completes.
- **Et Tu, Brute?** (2 copies, value 3): A versatile whose value increases for each friendly fighter adjacent to the target. Often his highest-value block.
- **Again** (3 copies, value 3): An Actor-only versatile that lets you return the first card of your line to your hand. Use it to fix a broken syllable count or recur a valuable card.

### Tips

- Completion effects happen even on the opponent's turn. Use this to trigger *Once More Unto The Breach* or *Deceive* on defense.
- Use *Deceive* defensively to complete a line, returning it to your hand so you still hold a cancel for their next attack.
- Because "Any" cards can be played by anyone, let your Actors attack with cards like *All Are Punished* to maintain offensive pressure while keeping Shakespeare safely away from counterattacks.
- The sources disagree on *The Ides of March*: one recommends completing lines with it to deal unblockable damage and bait strong defenses, while another calls it the worst card in the deck because its base value of 2 with no effect is too weak to risk playing.
- Smaller maps with pockets (like Raptor Paddock or Globe Theatre) suit him best, allowing him to attack without boosting and safely hide behind his Actors.
