## Oda Nobunaga

**In short:** A highly aggressive, positioning-heavy melee fighter. His strategy revolves almost entirely around his two Honor Guards, turning them into your primary win condition by setting up flanking traps and attacking with his zone buff.

### Plan A: Trap and crush

**Goal:** Pin the opponent with flanking positioning and hit them with enhanced Honor Guard attacks.
**How to do it:** An opposing fighter is flanked when they are adjacent to two or more of your fighters. Move the Honor Guards into Oda's zone so they receive his +1 value buff, then surround the enemy. Flanking unlocks your most devastating attacks. Sun and Moon ignores the opponent's card value, making it an unblockable 3 damage (with the buff) unless they cancel it. Lightning and Thunder cancels their effects and lets your fighter move up to 2 spaces away after combat. This creates a trap: opponents want to play a cancel against Sun and Moon, but will take heavy damage if they guess wrong and you play the high-value Lightning and Thunder instead. Meanwhile, Fire and Flames jumps to a base 5 value when flanking, swinging for a massive 6 if played by a buffed sidekick.
**Watch out for:** You only have three bodies on the board. If even one Honor Guard dies, flanking becomes incredibly difficult. If both die, almost half the cards in your deck lose their primary effects.

### Plan B: Draw heavily to maintain the assault

**Goal:** Dig for your flanking tools and play your best cards before the Honor Guards are killed.
**How to do it:** Your sidekicks have weak defenses, so they will rarely survive a long match. Attack relentlessly and draw rapidly through your deck to maximize their damage output while they are alive. Student of War draws a card for every point of damage dealt, loading your hand for continuous assaults. The Reinforce scheme offers flexible options, but choosing to draw two cards and gain an action is the ideal way to keep up the pressure. On defense, Battle Maneuvers draws a card and lets you move each of your fighters up to 2 spaces, allowing you to position a perfect flank during their turn and leaving your actions free to strike on yours.
**Watch out for:** Be extremely careful playing Student of War against a 1-health sidekick. If the opponent decides not to defend, you will deal the full 5 damage and draw five cards, heavily accelerating your fatigue clock.

### Plan C: Swap to spread the damage

**Goal:** Keep both Honor Guards alive for as long as possible to maintain your flanking threat.
**How to do it:** Since your sidekicks share the damage burden, keeping both on the board is vital. Spring the Trap lets the defender swap spaces with an adjacent friendly fighter, who then takes the attack instead (and deals 1 damage back after combat). Use this to step a wounded Honor Guard out of danger and let a healthy one, or Oda himself, take the hit. Momentous Shift provides your guards with their highest printed block of 3.
**Watch out for:** The sources disagree on the versatile card Patience and Strategy, which heals your fighter for 2 if the opponent is flanked. One praises it as a way to bait out strong blocks while healing. Another argues it rarely works defensively because opponents will simply maneuver away from the flank before attacking, and playing it on your turn wastes an action needed to deal real damage.

### How they lose

- Opponents will target the Honor Guards first. Once they drop, Oda has nobody to buff and zero flanking enablers, leaving him virtually defenseless and unable to secure knockouts.
- Automatic damage abilities are a nightmare for this team. Pragmatism is your only defensive cancel and strictly locked to Oda, meaning the Honor Guards have absolutely no way to stop effects during a defense.
- Fast opponents who constantly run away expose Oda's lack of mobility. There are only five cards with a boost value of 3 in the entire deck, making it incredibly tough to close the gap against evasive fighters.

### Key cards

- **Student of War**: your biggest attack for reloading your hand and maintaining pressure.
- **Lightning and Thunder**: your offensive cancel and escape tool.
- **Sun and Moon**: a cheap, virtually unblockable strike when you have the enemy flanked.
- **Battle Maneuvers**: essential free movement to set up traps without spending actions.
- **Reinforce**: the engine that keeps your momentum flowing.

### Tips

- Treat your Honor Guards as your primary attackers, not expendable pawns.
- Save your 3-boost cards strictly for movement against opponents who run away. While the scheme Demon King of the Sixth Heaven deals 2 damage to flanked enemies, sources advise against playing it for its text since catching multiple fighters in a flank is rare. It is much more valuable as a boost.
- Always check that an Honor Guard is standing inside Oda's zone before you play their card to ensure they receive the +1 buff.
- When using Spring the Trap to swap fighters, calculate your zones carefully: if the new defender steps into a space outside of Oda's zone, they instantly lose their defense bonus for that combat.
