## The Golden Bat

**In short:** A highly mobile, tanky melee fighter who forces opponents to chase him, then hits back with massive values. He bypasses the need to maneuver by moving with schemes and card effects, ensuring his ability triggers on his attacks. The sources disagree on his playstyle: one describes him as a straightforward hero for beginners, while another argues his true strength lies in a calculated, frustrating game of running away to exhaust the opponent's deck.

### Plan A: Kite and outvalue them

**Goal:** Force the opponent to spend their cards reaching you, hit them hard, and immediately escape.
**How to do it:** His ability adds +2 to his attacks if he hasn't taken a Maneuver action this turn. Rather than chasing the opponent, stand your ground. Let them spend their movement and boost cards to reach you. On your turn, attack with a versatile card like Skirmish (2 copies, value 4) or Like a Flash of Golden Light (3 copies, value 2). With your ability, these hit for up to 6. If you win the combat, their effects allow you to move away—up to 5 spaces with Like a Flash of Golden Light. The opponent is left far away and must waste more cards boosting to catch you again. Over time, you build a massive card advantage because they burn their deck just trying to keep up.
**Watch out for:** Fighters with high mobility or extra actions (such as the Raptors or Houdini). They can easily close the distance and attack multiple times without needing to boost, completely disrupting this hit-and-run rhythm.

### Plan B: Loop the Alpine Fortress

**Goal:** Keep a constant stream of cards and movement without ever maneuvering.
**How to do it:** Alpine Fortress (3 copies) is the engine that drives his deck. It shuffles a card from your discard pile into your deck, draws 2 cards, and moves you 4 spaces. Because it is a scheme, it bridges the gap to the enemy without spending a Maneuver action, securing your +2 attack bonus for your second action. Shuffling a second copy of Alpine Fortress into your deck lets you repeat this cycle endlessly. If you are playing for exhaustion, you can shuffle Arrive Just in Time into your deck instead to warp around the map, forcing the opponent to overdraw while you preserve your cards.
**Watch out for:** He has very little natural card draw outside of this scheme. If an aggressive opponent forces you to spend your hand defending before you draw an Alpine Fortress, you can quickly run out of options.

### Plan C: Trap them for massive damage

**Goal:** Ensure your heaviest, uncancelable attacks connect.
**How to do it:** Super Strength (2 copies, value 5) has no printed effect, meaning a Feint cannot stop it. Combined with your ability, it becomes a devastating 7. To make sure the opponent cannot simply run out of your range before you use it, trap them. Terrifying Roar (3 copies, value 3) stops fighters from leaving their spaces for the rest of the turn. Play it as your first attack to lock them down, then follow up with Super Strength on your second action. You can also play Terrifying Roar on defense to stop an opponent's hit-and-run tactics, trapping them adjacent to you so they are perfectly positioned for your turn.
**Watch out for:** Super Strength and your 5-value block, He Laughs at Your Feebleness, win combats purely on value. They offer no protection against attacks that deal damage through card effects, meaning fighters who rely on after-combat damage can still hurt you.

### How they lose

​- Relentless aggressive pressure breaks his defenses. He has no healing, and once his strongest blocks are gone, he struggles to refill his hand.
​- He relies heavily on his 5-value block to stay alive, making him very vulnerable to uncancelable effect damage (such as Little Red's Once Upon a Time).
​- Opponents who fight effectively from range never trigger his hit-and-run cycle. If they refuse to approach, he is forced to spend his own actions to reach them, disabling his ability.
​- Card effects that cancel his movement leave him stranded. Since he relies on combat wins to escape, a well-timed block from the opponent traps him in melee range.

### Key cards

​- **Alpine Fortress**: his best card. It recycles his tools, draws replacements, and perfectly positions him to trigger his ability.
​- **Like a Flash of Golden Light**: his main kiting tool. It becomes a 4 if you share no zones with the space you started in. Combined with your ability, it hits for 6 and rockets you safely across the board.
​- **Vaporizing Eyebeams**: a 2-value attack that makes the opponent discard a random card. Thrown as a 4 with your ability, it forces them to either take the damage or waste a high-value block just to stop the discard effect.
​- **Super Strength**: a blank attack that threatens an uncancelable 7.
​- **Imposing Presence**: a 3-value defense that cancels all effects. As his only cancel, it is vital for stopping enemy movement tricks and combo chains.

### Tips

​- Do not be afraid to double maneuver and discard cards at the end of your turn. Golden Bat has several cards with very weak effects, like A Punch to Shake the Earth and Sight Beyond Sight. Discarding them forces your opponent to overdraw and discard their own, often much better, cards.
​- Use A Punch to Shake the Earth against fighters with multiple 1-health sidekicks (like Wukong or Robin Hood). The area damage clears them out without you needing to win individual combats.
​- Look for map spaces that force ranged attackers to stand adjacent to you to gain line of sight. This ensures they end their turn in melee range, handing you an easy +2 attack.
​- Play Arrive Just in Time as your first action to warp next to an enemy, leaving your second action open for a boosted attack.
​- Insight of the Ancients returns a random card from your discard pile to your hand, not your deck. Play it early when your discard pile is mostly strong cards to ensure you get something valuable back.
