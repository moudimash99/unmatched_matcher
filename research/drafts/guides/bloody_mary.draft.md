## Bloody Mary

**In short:** A fast solo melee fighter who plays a precise hand-management game. By curating her hand down to exactly three cards, she earns a third action to unleash heavy attacks, but she must constantly bait out cancels to make her biggest hits connect.

### Plan A: Manage the three-card hand

**Goal:** Start your turn with exactly three cards in hand to trigger her ability.
**How to do it:** Reaching exactly three cards requires careful maneuvering and blocking. Play aggressively to empty your hand, and avoid double maneuvering when you want to keep your hand size low. Ending your turn with exactly four cards puts the opponent in a difficult position: if they attack you once and you block, you drop to three cards and gain your extra action on the next turn. When they do attack, use Evade or Broken Glass. Both cards replace themselves with a card draw after combat, keeping your hand size exactly where it was before the attack.
**Watch out for:** Fighters with hand disruption (like Loki) or forced discards can ruin your precise count. Because your hand is small, you also have fewer options when choosing how to defend against a sudden heavy hit.

### Plan B: Bait out the cancels

**Goal:** Clear the opponent's Feints so your third-action effects can resolve.
**How to do it:** Her third-action attacks are highly telegraphed. An experienced opponent will hold their Feints specifically for the third action to reduce Speak Three Times to a mere 3 damage. You have to draw those Feints out early. Attack with Jump Scare (a 6-value if you changed zones) or Bloody Requiem (which forces them to spend a second card to block the Bloody Reprise). Because these are massive threats on action one or two, opponents are forced to spend valuable blocks or even Feints on them. Alternatively, you can use a third action to attack with a weak card like Trick of the Light or Ghostly Touch, tricking the opponent into wasting their cancel on a harmless attack.
**Watch out for:** The sources disagree on how strictly to pursue her ability. Most recommend carefully managing your hand to trigger the third action (small hand Mary). However, some players advocate for "big hand Mary," completely ignoring her passive ability and simply rushing the opponent with a full hand to overwhelm them.

### Plan C: Set up the perfect turn

**Goal:** Ensure your biggest finisher lands safely when you finally get three actions.
**How to do it:** Use your first two actions to gather information and maneuver into position. Stolen Memories lets you look at the opponent's hand. Even if they choose to take damage to keep their best card, you now know exactly what defenses and cancels they are holding. If the coast is clear, drop Speak Three Times on your third action for a full 7 damage. When you have a large hand and cannot trigger your passive ability, Closer Than She Appears is your shortcut. It grants a move, a card, and an action, letting you artificially manufacture a third action without needing the strict three-card setup.
**Watch out for:** Stolen Memories gives the opponent the choice to discard the card or take its boost value as damage. They will almost always choose to take a small ping of damage to keep a crucial Feint, so the card acts more as an information tool than true discard.

### How they lose

​- She has only two copies of Feint, leaving her highly vulnerable to uncancelable damage and nasty opponent effects.
​- As a solo melee fighter with no sidekicks, she has low board presence and can be easily body-blocked or pinned.
​- Opponents with multiple cancels (like Bigfoot) can simply hold them all game exclusively for her third actions.
​- Mirror Image only reads the printed value of the opponent's card. It fails entirely against fighters who rely on card effects, abilities, or boosts to increase their damage (like Medusa's Second Shot).
​- Fast opponents with extra actions can hit her multiple times in one turn, easily overwhelming her small hand.

### Key cards

​- **Speak Three Times**: her best finisher. A 3-value attack that becomes a 7 on your third action.
​- **Closer Than She Appears**: a scheme that grants an action. Your most reliable way to secure a third action when your hand is too full to trigger her ability.
​- **Broken Glass**: a flexible 3-value versatile that can shift up or down by 1. If its final value matches the opponent's card, you deal 2 direct damage and draw a card. Excellent for punishing standard 3- or 4-value blocks.
​- **Jump Scare**: a 3-value versatile that hits for 6 if you share no zones with the space you started your turn in. Keep the threat of this card active to make opponents nervous.
​- **Evade**: a 3-value defense that draws a card. Crucial for blocking without shrinking your hand size.

### Tips

​- End your turns near zone borders. This makes it easy to step into a new zone on your next turn and immediately activate Jump Scare.
​- Play Out Of The Mirror on your first or second action. You miss the card draw, but you still get the random discard and the boost value, and you avoid wasting it into a third-action Feint.
​- Bloody Requiem is great in the early game. Forcing the opponent to defend the initial attack and the 0-value Bloody Reprise strips valuable blocks from their hand.
​- If you start your turn with one or two cards, double maneuver and use your boosts to run away, forcing the opponent to waste their own cards boosting just to reach you.
​- Do not waste your two Feints on small hits; save them to break opponent combos or stop game-ending effects.
