## Michelangelo

**In short:** An unpredictable, highly aggressive melee fighter hindered by a tiny hand limit. His unique ability forces him to draw a card whenever he attacks or schemes, fueling rapid deck cycling and allowing him to string together action-granting cards for massive combo turns.

### Plan A: Chain your actions

**Goal:** Play multiple cards in a single turn to overwhelm the opponent and build up a massive finishing attack.
**How to do it:** Many of your attacks naturally extend your turn. Boisterous beatdown (value 2) grants an action after combat, or two actions if you win the combat. Hi-yaaaaah!! (value 4) and April's Hard-hitting investigation (value 0) also provide action gains. Since your ability dictates that you draw a card whenever you attack or play a scheme, these options continually replace themselves. Keep stringing these actions together until you draw a massive final hit. Cowabunga!! (value 3) gains power for every other card you previously played that turn, making it the perfect capstone to a long offensive chain. Turtle power! (value 3) serves as another heavy hitter because it can be boosted. The scheme Nunchaku gives all your attacks a +1 bonus for the rest of the turn, grants an action, and replaces itself, scaling your combo even higher.
**Watch out for:** Defensive cancels. If an opponent feints your first action-gaining attack, your combo momentum dies instantly. Open with your weaker attacks to bait out their cancels before committing your biggest hits to a long chain.

### Plan B: Cycle the small hand

**Goal:** Manage your strict hand limit to find your combo pieces without overdrawing or wasting cards.
**How to do it:** You begin the game with only three cards and must discard down to three at the end of every turn. With such tight limitations, you must constantly play cards to avoid discarding them. Hi-yaaaaah!!, the versatile cancel Guaranteed delivery (value 3), and Shell insertion all allow you to place a card from your hand on the bottom of your deck. This creates room in your hand before your mandatory ability draws trigger, preserving important cards for later. If using Guaranteed delivery leaves your hand entirely empty, it draws two cards after combat to immediately refuel you.
**Watch out for:** The sources differ on the required planning. One suggests simply throwing whatever you draw because the strict hand limit prevents real long-term strategy, while another emphasizes carefully managing your hand and tucking specific cards to set up continuous assault loops.

### How they lose

- He burns through his deck first. The mandatory draw on every attack and scheme becomes a fatal liability when the deck runs out, dealing exhaustion damage every time he takes the offensive.
- Relentless aggression overwhelms him. With a maximum of three cards in hand, he struggles to hold enough defensive options to survive multiple heavy attacks in a single round.
- Mobile opponents kite him. If forced to maneuver and burn cards on boosts just to close the distance, he wastes his few resources and ends up empty-handed when he finally engages.
- His action gains get canceled early in a turn, stopping his combo potential before Cowabunga!! can scale up.

### Key cards

- **Cowabunga!!**: The primary combo finisher. A 3-value versatile that gains +1 for every card you previously played that turn.
- **Hi-yaaaaah!!**: A heavy 4-value attack. It deals 1 damage to an adjacent opposing fighter and lets you tuck a card to gain an action.
- **Guaranteed delivery**: His sole defensive cancel. Activating it requires tucking a card, and it replenishes your hand with two cards after combat if your hand is left empty.
- **Hard-hitting investigation**: April's 0-value attack that pings the opposing fighter for 1 damage and grants an action.
- **Shell insertion**: April's scheme offering four different effects. Players usually pick the options to tuck a card for an action and to shuffle two random cards from the discard pile back into the deck.

### Tips

- Avoid double maneuvering just to flee. Since attacking inherently draws a card, it is usually better to attack and force the opponent to burn a defense; it refreshes your hand just like maneuvering, but drains their resources.
- Trap the opponent against the edge of the board using both fighters so they cannot simply retreat from your combo turns.
- Target weak sidekicks with Boisterous beatdown. If you win the combat against a 1-health sidekick, the card yields two actions instead of one.
- Play mind games with April. Opponents often expect her 0-value action-gainer and willingly accept the hit. You can punish this by throwing Guaranteed delivery on offense instead to sneak 3 damage through.
- Stack your damage boosts. Playing two copies of Nunchaku in a single turn grants +2 to all attacks. If you then recur them with Shell insertion, you can theoretically draw and replay them for even higher modifiers.
- If you go first, push immediately. With the right opening hand, you can chain enough action gains together to potentially win the game on turn one.
