## George Washington

**In short:** A defensive melee fighter who relies on a psychological bluffing game. He forces opponents into bad choices with his ruse tokens, and uses his network of 1-health Culper Spies to power up his key cards.

### Plan A: Bluff with ruse tokens

**Goal:** Force the opponent to waste cards or take heavy damage by guessing wrong.
**How to do it:** His special ability lets you place one of your four ruse tokens on an attack. Before defending, the opponent can discard a random card to remove it. Because actual ruse effects are strong, you can bluff by putting tokens on regular cards to keep the opponent scared. Lead Astray (2 copies, value 5) is the perfect bait. If you attach a ruse token to it and the opponent plays a Feint to stop a perceived ruse effect, they take 3 damage. If they discard a random card or choose not to cancel, you can use Lead Astray's effect to move 2 spaces and safely disengage.
**Watch out for:** The sources disagree on how to spend ruse tokens. Some recommend using them to bluff because regaining them is easy and it maintains psychological pressure. Another argues you should save tokens strictly for cards that have real ruse effects to guarantee you get their full value.

### Plan B: Keep the Spies alive for value

**Goal:** Maintain your board presence to enable your best schemes and boosts.
**How to do it:** Washington's three Culper Spies have 1 health each, but they are essential to his deck. You need a living Spy on the board to play the scheme Sympathetic Stain, and Network Of Spies gets a +1 value boost for each friendly fighter in Washington's zone. Keep the Spies spread out across different zones so a single area-of-effect attack cannot clear them all at once.
**Watch out for:** Once they are gone, he struggles to bring them back. He only has two copies of Recruit To The Ring to revive defeated Spies.

### How they lose

​- He lacks raw damage output and relies on getting specific combat interactions. If his Circumvents miss high blocks, or his Lead Astrays don't land into Feints, he won't deal enough damage to win.
​- He only has two passes to stall his deck, so his fatigue game is just average. He struggles against stronger fatigue fighters when he is forced to chase them, like Beowulf.
​- He sacrifices his Spies too early, losing the ability to play his spy-dependent cards.

### Key cards

​- **Lead Astray**: a 5-value attack that moves a fighter up to 2 spaces. His best bait for a ruse token.
​- **Sympathetic Stain**: a scheme that lets you look at their hand, force a discard, and regain a ruse token, but only if a Spy is adjacent to an enemy.
​- **Circumvent**: a 3-value attack that ignores the opponent's card value. Use it to punch through big blocks.
​- **Undercover Agent**: a 1-value defense that swaps Washington with a Spy so the sidekick takes the hit.
​- **Recruit To The Ring**: a 2-value versatile, and his only way to return a defeated Spy to the board.

### Tips

​- Hold off on playing Undercover Agent early in the game. You want to keep your Spies alive to get value from Sympathetic Stain before throwing them in front of an attack.
​- Regain spent ruse tokens by winning combats with Misinformation or playing Sympathetic Stain, which keeps the bluffing engine running.
