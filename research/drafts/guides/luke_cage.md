## Luke Cage

**In short:** A tough melee fighter who reduces incoming combat damage by 2 and hits back with massive attacks. He controls the tempo of the game by threatening to end the opponent's turn early, but he struggles with low mobility and effect damage.

### Plan A: Cut their turn short and hit back

**Goal:** Stop their offensive combos and catch them next to you for a double attack.
**How to do it:** Luke's ability reduces incoming combat damage by 2, meaning his 2-value blocks effectively absorb 4 damage. Trash Talk is a 2-value defense that cancels effects and, if you win the combat, ends the opponent's turn. Played by Luke, it usually wins against standard attacks. If an opponent attacks you on their first action, Trash Talk leaves them stranded adjacent to you with no second action to run away. On your turn, punish them with massive attacks like Sweet Christmas! (value 6) or Commanding Impact (value 5).
**Watch out for:** Experienced opponents will maneuver up to you and refuse to attack, trying to bait out your defenses or force you to act first. Others will rely on ranged fighters to avoid standing next to you when their turn ends.

### Plan B: Loop the deck

**Goal:** Outlast the exhaustion clock and wear the opponent down with repeated high-value cards.
**How to do it:** Still Standing is a 4-value attack that lets you shuffle two cards from your discard pile back into your deck if you win the combat. Use it to recover cards like Trash Talk or Sweet Christmas!. When you play the second copy of Still Standing, you can choose the first copy as one of the returned cards, creating an infinite deck loop. Play the first copy early in the game. This forces the opponent to spend their highest blocks on every subsequent attack, because they never know when the second copy is coming. Misty can play it from range, making it even harder to predict.
**Watch out for:** The effect requires winning the combat, so a 4-value block or a cancel stops the loop.

### Plan C: Punish them for hitting you

**Goal:** Turn their biggest attacks and sidekicks against them.
**How to do it:**

- Skin Like Titanium (value None) deals the combat damage you take back to the attacker. Use it against massive printed attacks, like Bigfoot's 6s. You both take heavy damage, but you can immediately follow up with your own big hits. Do this early while you still have your 13 health to spend.
- Power Man (value 2) moves the attacker up to 3 spaces and deals 1 damage to them and each opposing fighter they move through. This instantly clears 1-health sidekicks like Wukong's clones if you push the attacker through them.

**Watch out for:** You only have 13 health. Using Skin Like Titanium when you are low on health will kill you before you can counterattack.

### How they lose

- His ability only reduces combat damage. Fighters with heavy effect damage (like Invisible Man or Little Red) or auto-damage bypass his defenses entirely.
- He has a move of 2 and only five cards with a boost of 3 (Regroup and Pushback). Highly mobile fighters can kite him on large maps, waiting for his hand to empty.
- Misty dies early. She provides ranged pressure and draws out attacks. Without her, the opponent can safely manage their distance.
- His deck is heavily skewed toward attacks. If he draws a hand full of red cards, he is left completely exposed without defensive options.

### Key cards

- **Trash Talk**: A vital defense. Despite ending the turn on a win, end-of-turn effects (like Bigfoot's card draw or Moon Knight's phase shift) still resolve.
- **Where's My Money?**: A scheme that places Luke adjacent to the nearest opposing fighter and gives an action. His best tool to close the gap against solo heroes.
- **Still Standing**: Your recursion engine.
- **Daughter of the Dragon**: A 2-value versatile for Misty. If she is adjacent to the attacker, it becomes a 6. Use it to keep her alive against melee attackers, or walk up and attack with it to draw out a cancel.
- **Got My Back?**: A 1-value defense that swaps Misty with Luke, making him the defender. Keep her alive by letting him take the hit with his damage reduction.

### Tips

- Luke's damage reduction applies even if he doesn't play a defense card or if a card effect ignores his printed defense value.
- Regroup acts as a 3-value block for Luke because of his damage reduction. Because his ability states he wins combat if he takes no damage, you will often win and draw two cards, refilling your hand quickly.
- Hero For Hire (value 3) lets you boost the attack during combat. Wait to see what the opponent plays; if they drop a high-value defense, save your boost cards and let the attack fail.
- Be careful when boosting Hero For Hire or your maneuvers. Pushback and Regroup are your only 3-boost cards. If you throw them all away, you will struggle to catch opponents on large maps.
- Attack with Commanding Impact rather than Daughter of the Dragon when closing in with Misty. If they cancel Commanding Impact, it still deals 5 damage over the cancel.
- If two opposing fighters are exactly the same distance away when you play Where's My Money?, you get to choose which one to teleport next to.
