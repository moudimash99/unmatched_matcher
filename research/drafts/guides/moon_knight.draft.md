## Moon Knight

**In short:** A solo melee fighter who cycles through three identities on a strict timer. He sets up positioning with Moon Knight, hits for massive damage and ignores effects as Khonshu, and relies on Mr. Knight to repel counterattacks.

### Plan A: Master the rhythm

**Goal:** Line up your best cards with the identity that maximizes them.
**How to do it:** You are locked into changing identities at the end of every turn: Moon Knight to Khonshu to Mr. Knight, and back again.
​- **Moon Knight** is your setup phase. His ability moves him up to 2 spaces at the start of your turn for free. Use this to retreat and draw cards safely, or to close the gap without spending an action to maneuver.
​- **Khonshu** is your attack phase. He adds +2 to his attack cards, making him your primary damage dealer. Set up double attacks by starting your turn adjacent to the opponent, or use Travelers of The Night to close in.
​- **Mr. Knight** is your defense phase. He adds +1 to all his defense values. Because he follows Khonshu in the cycle, you will usually be standing next to an angry opponent who just took a massive hit. He exists to survive that counterattack.

**Watch out for:** Opponents know the cycle. They will run away before your Khonshu turn, forcing you to waste your big attacks or spend your turn maneuvering. If you push too hard and get caught empty-handed as Moon Knight, you have no combat bonuses to save you.

### Plan B: Break the cycle

**Goal:** Catch the opponent off guard when they expect you to be vulnerable or harmless.
**How to do it:** I'm Not Real (3 copies, value 4) is an attack that lets you change to your next identity after combat, bypassing the end-of-turn rule. Play it as your first action as Moon Knight to immediately become Khonshu, letting you swing with a +2 bonus on your second action. Defending with it as Mr. Knight skips your vulnerable Moon Knight turn completely, pushing you straight to Khonshu at the end of your turn.
**Watch out for:** The change happens after combat, so the attack itself uses your current identity's stats. I'm Not Real played by Moon Knight only hits for 4, not 6.

### Plan C: Abuse Khonshu's immunities

**Goal:** Leverage self-damage cards and ignore exhaustion.
**How to do it:** Khonshu does not take damage from effects other than combat damage. This means you can play cards with heavy self-damage penalties entirely for free. Good Enough For Us lets you draw 2 cards and take 2 damage; as Khonshu, you get the cards and ignore the damage. He also ignores outside effect damage, completely negating Medusa's end-of-turn ping or Ghost Rider's Hellfire if they trigger while he is active. If the game goes to exhaustion, Khonshu takes zero fatigue damage from an empty deck.
**Watch out for:** Mr. Knight and Moon Knight are not immune to effects. Opponents will save their auto-damage cards (like Little Red's Once Upon a Time) for those identities.

### How they lose

​- Aggressive fighters overwhelm him while he is Moon Knight, when he has no value boosts and no effect immunities.
​- Fighters with sidekicks body block his paths. Without Travelers of The Night, Khonshu wastes his turn hitting 1-health targets instead of the enemy hero.
​- Fast opponents kite him relentlessly, forcing him to spend his Khonshu turns maneuvering instead of attacking.
​- He runs out of cards trying to keep up pressure. The deck only has two reliable card draw options, and losing one hurts his tempo.

### Key cards

​- **That's The Part I Like**: a 3-value versatile. If you win combat, you look at the top 3 cards of their deck, discard one, and put the rest back. As Khonshu, this is a 5-value attack that destroys their best card before they ever draw it, and gives you knowledge of their next two turns.
​- **Past and Present Intermingle**: a 2-value versatile that adds the boost value of the top card of your discard pile. Keep track of what you played last; your two scheme cards have a boost of 3, making this up to a 5 for Moon Knight, a 7 for Khonshu, or a 6 for Mr. Knight.
​- **Travelers of The Night**: moves you 4 spaces through enemies and gives an action. Your best tool to bypass sidekicks and secure a double attack on a Khonshu turn.
​- **That's Why I Always Win**: a 3-value attack you may boost. With a 3-value boost card, this hits for 6. With Khonshu's +2, it hits for 8.
​- **Good Enough For Us**: a 4-value versatile that draws 2 cards for 2 damage. Use it with Khonshu to negate the penalty, or with Mr. Knight as a 5-value block that reloads your hand.

### Tips

​- Never play Let Your Insanity Guide You (discard the top card of your deck to draw cards equal to its boost). It randomly throws away your best cards and pushes you toward exhaustion. Use it only as a 2-value boost for movement or attacks.
​- Feint becomes a 4-value attack for Khonshu or a 3-value block for Mr. Knight, making it much stronger than a standard 2-value Feint.
​- Play Madness Will Keep You Alive after moving with Moon Knight's free start-of-turn spaces. Because you started the turn in a different space, you recover 2 health and immediately gain the action back without having to spend an action to maneuver first.
​- The sources disagree on A Totally Sane Thing To Do (deals 2 damage to both fighters): one prefers using it as an attack with Khonshu to deal unblockable extra damage, while another argues it is better played as a surprise defense with Khonshu to deal 2 damage back to the attacker while taking none yourself.
​- Keep your discard pile in strict order. Past and Present Intermingle depends entirely on the exact top card.
