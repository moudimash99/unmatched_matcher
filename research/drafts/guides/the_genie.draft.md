## The Genie

**In short:** A highly versatile ranged fighter who uses cards as a resource. He discards cards for extra actions, draws heavily to maintain an advantage, and uses ping damage to wear down opponents before unleashing a massive combo turn.

### Plan A: The discard combo

**Goal:** Empty the opponent's hand and finish them with undefended attacks.
**How to do it:** Infinite Power lets you discard a card at the start of your turn to gain an action. Wait until the opponent ends their turn with six or fewer cards. Use your ability to gain a third action, then play all three copies of Three Wishes (scheme, 3 copies). Choose the effect that makes each opponent discard two cards. They discard six cards total, leaving them with an empty hand. Attack them with your remaining actions using cards like Careful What You Wish For (4-value, 3 copies), which they can no longer block.
**Watch out for:** This is an all-in move. It requires you to have a nearly full hand before starting so you still have attacks left after playing the wishes. If you attempt this against a high-health fighter like She-Hulk or Alice and fail to kill them, you are left with no cards and no more wishes for the rest of the game.

### Plan B: Aggressive pressure

**Goal:** Overwhelm the opponent with continuous attacks to force them out of defenses.
**How to do it:** Instead of hoarding Three Wishes for discards, use one or two to draw five cards. This fuels your hand, allowing you to safely use Infinite Power every turn to attack twice or three times. Fighters with low defense quantities or those who need to manage their own resources (like Dracula or Beowulf) will struggle to keep up. When you set your card values to 4 with a Three Wishes, you guarantee heavy hits. Turn weak cards like This is No Parlor Trick (1-value, 2 copies) into 4-value attacks. Because your attacks are consistent fours, you force the opponent to spend their best blocks quickly.
**Watch out for:** Drawing five cards repeatedly accelerates the exhaustion clock. If the opponent survives your initial burst, you will run out of cards and take fatigue damage long before they do.

### Plan C: Patient value and chip damage

**Goal:** Slowly drain the opponent's health and resources without overcommitting.
**How to do it:** Use I've Made Sultans Out of Less (2-value versatile, 2 copies) to look at their hand and force them to discard a card of your choice. This removes their best defense or combo piece and gives you perfect information for your next turn. Keep your distance and use I Am Freed (3-value attack, 2 copies) to deal 1 damage to an adjacent fighter, then teleport to any empty space on the board. This forces melee fighters to waste boosts just to reach you. You can safely clear 1-health sidekicks with this teleport or with I Grant You Death (2-value versatile, 3 copies). When they finally reach you, refill your hand on defense with Wishing For More Wishes (3-value versatile, 3 copies), which draws you three cards.
**Watch out for:** Your printed defense values are very low. Playing a patient game means you will take damage, and because you are a solo fighter with 16 health, chip damage adds up against you quickly.

### How they lose

- You deck out. Drawing up to 15 cards with Three Wishes, combined with the heavy discard costs of your ability and attacks, brings you to exhaustion faster than almost anyone.
- Your defenses top out at 3. Big printed attacks easily punch through your blocks.
- Back In The Lamp is a 0-value defense. While it heals you immediately, it guarantees you lose the combat. This triggers harmful effects on cards like Winter Soldier's Born in the Barracks or Luke Cage's attacks.
- Opponents aggressively maintain a hand size of seven, making the discard combo turn impossible to pull off completely.
- Fast, relentless attackers don't give you the time to build up the hand size required for a combo turn.

### Key cards

- **Three Wishes**: Your entire engine. It gives an action and a choice: draw 5 cards, set your card values to 4 for the turn, or make the opponent discard 2.
- **I've Made Sultans Out of Less**: Chosen discard is rare and powerful. It eliminates their best card and reveals exactly how to plan your wishes.
- **Back In The Lamp**: A 0-value defense that immediately recovers 4 health. Because the healing happens immediately, it cannot be canceled by a Feint. It acts similarly to a 4-value block against small attacks, but costs you the combat victory.
- **Prisoner's Torment**: A 1-value defense that lets you draw cards equal to the combat damage you took. The sources disagree on this: some see it as a terrible trade that drains your low health, while others consider it an underrated engine fuel that heavily sets up a massive combo turn.
- **This is No Parlor Trick**: A 1-value versatile that sets the opponent's card value to its boost value during combat. It is highly situational on defense and often better used as discard fuel for your ability, or as an attack when Three Wishes sets it to 4.

### Tips

- Discard This is No Parlor Trick for your Infinite Power ability first. Its defense effect is risky and frequently increases the opponent's attack value.
- Attack with Feint (2-value, 3 copies) on turns when you use Three Wishes to set your card values to 4. A 4-value attack that cancels defensive effects is brutal.
- Before attempting the discard combo, use I've Made Sultans Out of Less on defense to lower their hand size and check if they hold crucial cancels.
- Use I Am Freed to teleport back next to an opponent who just used a defensive card to move away.
- Do not use Back In The Lamp if you are at 13 or more health. You cannot recover health past your maximum of 16, turning it into a straight 0-value block.
