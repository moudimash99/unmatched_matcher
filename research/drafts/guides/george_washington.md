## George Washington

**In short:** A defensive melee fighter who relies on a psychological bluffing game. He forces opponents into bad choices with his ruse tokens, and uses his network of 1-health Culper Spies to power up his key cards.

### Plan A: Bluff with ruse tokens

**Goal:** Force the opponent to waste cards or take heavy damage by guessing wrong.
**How to do it:** His special ability lets you place one of your four ruse tokens on an attack. Before defending, the opponent can discard a random card to remove it. You can bluff by putting tokens on regular attacks to keep the opponent scared of powerful ruse effects. Lead Astray (2 copies, 5 value) is perfect bait. If you use a token on it and they play a Feint to cancel what they assume is a ruse, they take 3 damage. If they discard a card or choose not to cancel, you can use the card's effect to move up to 2 spaces and safely disengage. Alternatively, against opponents with lots of cancels, play cards that actually have ruse abilities without spending a token to trick them into wasting their defenses.
**Watch out for:** Players disagree on token management. Some prefer aggressive bluffing because tokens are easy to regain, maintaining psychological pressure. Others argue you should save them strictly to guarantee your actual ruse effects trigger fully.

### Plan B: Keep the Spies alive for value

**Goal:** Maintain your board presence to enable your best schemes and boosts.
**How to do it:** Washington's three sidekicks are essential to his deck. Network Of Spies (versatile, 2 value) gains +1 value for each friendly fighter sharing Washington's zone, and his best scheme requires an active Spy. Keep your Spies spread across different zones so a single area-of-effect attack cannot eliminate them all at once.
**Watch out for:** Once they die, bringing them back is difficult. You only have two cards in the entire deck capable of reviving them.

### How they lose

- He lacks raw damage output and depends on specific combat interactions. If you cannot consistently land Circumvents into their high blocks, or bait Feints with your heavy attacks, you won't deal enough damage to win.
- With effectively only two passes to stall his deck, his fatigue game is limited. He struggles immensely if forced to chase down stronger fatigue fighters, like Beowulf.
- Sacrificing Spies too early leaves him unable to trigger his best sidekick-dependent abilities.

### Key cards

- **Lead Astray**: Your best bait for a ruse token, letting you hit hard or retreat.
- **Sympathetic Stain**: A scheme that lets you look at the opponent's hand. If a Spy is adjacent to one of their fighters, you also force them to discard a chosen card and you regain a ruse token.
- **Circumvent**: A 3-value attack that ignores the opponent's card value. Your primary way to punch through large defensive cards.
- **Undercover Agent**: A 1-value defense that lets Washington swap spaces with an adjacent Spy, forcing the sidekick to take the hit.
- **Recruit To The Ring**: A 2-value versatile card, and your only method for returning a defeated Spy to the board.

### Tips

- Hold off on playing Undercover Agent early in the game. You want to keep your sidekicks alive to get value from Sympathetic Stain before throwing them in front of an attack.
- Regain spent ruse tokens by winning combats with Misinformation or resolving a fully powered Sympathetic Stain, keeping your bluffing engine running.
