## Beowulf

**In short:** A bulky melee fighter who treats taking damage as a resource. He absorbs blows to generate Rage, which fuels uncancelable attacks, adaptable blocks, and healing, while relying on a deck full of passes to win the exhaustion game.

### Plan A: Manage the Rage economy

**Goal:** Maintain enough Rage to threaten powerful card effects without wasting the free tokens gained from being hit.
**How to do it:** Beowulf begins with one Rage and gains another every time he takes damage, capping at three. Try to end your turns holding one or two tokens instead of a full three. This forces opponents into an awkward dilemma: attack and hand you a free resource, or pass. To lean into this, do not over-block. It is often better to intentionally absorb a point or two of damage on defense to safely bank a token. When attacking with Epic Poem, an attack that immediately grants a token and gains power for each Rage you hold, plan the engagement so you have exactly two tokens beforehand. The immediate effect pushes you to the maximum, maximizing the attack to a five without wasting the benefit.
**Watch out for:** Spending resources randomly. Save them for high-impact turns. If the enemy refuses to swing and you urgently need fuel, the Vigor and Courage scheme forces a random discard and awards Rage equal to that card's boost.

### Plan B: Outlast them with exhaustion

**Goal:** Preserve your deck and make the opponent burn through their cards trying to catch you.
**How to do it:** Beowulf can pivot to a highly effective fatigue strategy. His deck contains seven schemes that do not force a draw, providing a wealth of passes to delay exhaustion. Since his movement is a sluggish two, resist the urge to discard cards to boost your mobility; force enemies to close the distance themselves. Use Wiglaf's nine health to body-block paths and soak up incoming attacks. When playing the Golden Drinking Horn scheme, prioritize recovering health or moving, and completely avoid the option to draw two cards so your deck stays thicker than your opponent's.
**Watch out for:** Fatal Struggle. Winning combat with this four-value attack forces you to draw two cards, accelerating your own fatigue. To turn the tables, play it when the opponent's hand is full so that if you lose, the penalty forces them to discard down. Alternatively, target a weak sidekick only when you actively want to draw.

### Plan C: Unleash uncancelable attacks

**Goal:** Deliver massive damage that bypasses defensive abilities.
**How to do it:** The Ancient Heirloom is your best offensive tool because its text cannot be canceled. You have the option to spend two Rage to raise its base value to a five, and another to boost it. If you cannot afford to do both, prioritize the boost option with a high-boost card like No Contest Expecteth. This creates a six-value attack for half the resource cost.
**Watch out for:** Sherlock Holmes. His Elementary defense ignores the incoming attack value instead of canceling the card's text. Burning your tokens to boost The Ancient Heirloom against him will still result in zero damage.

### How they lose

- He runs very few dedicated defense cards. Outside of his signature defensive tool, he must lean heavily on versatile cards to block.
- Massive, single-hit attacks bypass his bulk. Beowulf prefers taking minor hits to safely build his economy; massive spikes of damage burn through his seventeen health entirely too quickly.
- Savvy opponents can simply refuse to engage when his Rage is low, starving him of his central mechanic.

### Key cards

- **The Equal of Grendel**: a three-value defense card. For two tokens, its immediate effect deals the printed value of the incoming attack directly back to the opponent. Holding two Rage makes attackers terrified to throw their heaviest cards, like Bigfoot's Larger Than Life.
- **The Ancient Heirloom**: his premier uncancelable attack. It scales immensely if you have the resources to simultaneously increase its base value and boost it.
- **The War-King**: a one-value versatile. You can spend any amount of Rage to add two to its value per token. Because this decision happens during combat, it functions as a perfectly adaptable emergency shield.
- **Golden Drinking Horn**: a scheme allowing you to spend Rage for various effects (drawing, moving, or healing). You must select a different benefit for each token spent; you cannot choose to heal six health.
- **Remnant of Valor**: a Wiglaf scheme that inflicts one damage to every adjacent fighter. Catching Beowulf in the blast deals minimal friendly fire, but smoothly generates a Rage and refunds the action spent to play it.

### Tips

- Wiglaf lacks any dedicated defense cards. Hold onto your copies of the versatile Skirmish to keep him alive.
- Do not waste three Rage on the No Contest Expecteth attack effect to instantly defeat a sidekick, even a formidable one like Merlin or Patroclus. Its three boost value is much better spent powering up The Ancient Heirloom.
- Exploit enemies that deal automatic chip damage. Standing in Medusa's zone, for instance, translates her end-of-turn ability into free Rage.
- Use Hot for the Battle to strike with Wiglaf, then instantly trigger its after-combat effect to swap spaces, pulling him to safety while placing Beowulf on the frontline.
- Fighters who rely on chaining multiple attacks together, such as Bruce Lee or the Raptors, inadvertently play to your advantage. Because each strike is a separate instance of damage, they will rapidly max out your tokens.
