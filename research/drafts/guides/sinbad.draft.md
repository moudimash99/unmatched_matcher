## Sinbad

**In short:** A highly mobile melee fighter who starts weak but scales into an endgame powerhouse. He relies on playing or discarding his Voyage cards to ramp up his movement and their attack values, hitting and running while managing a dangerously fast-emptying deck.

### Plan A: Ramp up the Voyages

**Goal:** Get Voyage cards into your discard pile quickly to boost your movement and attack power.
**How to do it:** Every Voyage card in your discard pile adds 1 to your maneuver speed and 1 to the value of your other Voyage attacks. You want to cycle them into the discard pile as fast as possible. Play them early as weak attacks, knowing the opponent might just take the 2 damage rather than waste a block. If you end your turn with more than 7 cards, you can discard a Voyage directly from your hand. This safely adds it to your discard pile without risking a Feint. Prioritize playing or discarding the less critical ones early, like Voyage to the Valley of the Giant Snakes (look at their hand) or Voyage to the Cannibals With the Root of Madness (move 2).
**Watch out for:** Feints cancel both the boost from your discard pile and the after-combat effect, reducing the card to a flat 2 damage. Opponents will hoard their Feints for the late game when your Voyages are swinging for 6 or 7.

### Plan B: Hit, run, and block with versatiles

**Goal:** Use your massive movement advantage to strike safely, and carefully manage your lack of true defense cards.
**How to do it:** Once you have two or three Voyages down, your maneuver easily outpaces most fighters. You can step into range, attack, and then use the after-combat movement from Toil and Danger or Leap Away to retreat. This forces the opponent to waste their cards boosting just to reach you. With your high speed, Momentous Shift is practically a guaranteed 5-value attack since you will almost always start your turn in a different space. Use the Porter to clog up choke points so the opponent cannot reach Sinbad easily.
**Watch out for:** Sinbad has zero printed defense cards in his deck. Every time you use Exploit, Leap Away, or Toil and Danger on attack, you throw away a potential block. If you burn through your versatile cards too fast, you will be caught defenseless.

### Plan C: Reset the engine with Voyage Home

**Goal:** Reclaim your massive attacks to close out the game before exhaustion hits.
**How to do it:** Voyage Home takes all other Voyage cards from your discard pile and adds them to your hand. Suddenly, you have a hand full of attacks ready to launch again. Because pulling them out empties your discard pile, your movement and Voyage values temporarily reset to zero. To soften this, play Voyage Home when you already have a full hand. At the end of that turn, you will have too many cards and can immediately discard two or three of the weaker Voyages back into the discard pile, instantly regaining some of your buffs.
**Watch out for:** If an opponent plays a Feint against Voyage Home, you do not get the cards back. Experienced opponents anticipate this card and will save a Feint for it. Never play it as your absolute last Voyage; play it second or third to last, and try to draw out their Feints first.

### How they lose

​- He often beats himself through exhaustion. Between his two copies of Riches Beyond Compare, Exploit, By Fortune and Fate, and several Voyages, he has an incredible amount of card draw. He burns through his deck so fast that he risks taking fatal exhaustion damage before finishing off the enemy.
​- Fighters who pressure him relentlessly in the early game (like Bruce Lee or the Raptors) can drain his versatile blocks and health before his movement engine comes online.
​- Opponents with lots of cancels (Bigfoot has five) can Feint his mid-to-late Voyages, negating his biggest damage spikes.
​- Hand disruption hurts him deeply. A random discard effect can permanently take away Voyage Home or a crucial 4-value block.
​- His defense values top out at 4 (with just two copies of Leap Away and two of Exploit). Massive printed attacks punch right through his standard 3-value blocks.

### Key cards

​- **Voyage Home**: the centerpiece of his late game. It rescues your spent engine, but must be protected from Feints at all costs.
​- **Voyage to the City of the Man-Eating Apes**: deals 2 damage after combat. Brutal as a late-game attack, giving you guaranteed extra chip damage on top of a massive hit.
​- **Voyage to the Island That Was a Whale**: recovers 2 health. Since his health is strictly average and he has no other healing, save this until you've actually taken damage.
​- **Riches Beyond Compare**: a scheme that draws 3 cards. It digs you out of bad starting hands to find Voyages, but playing it late in the game actively pushes you closer to death by exhaustion.
​- **Toil and Danger**: a 3-value versatile that moves Sinbad up to 3 spaces. Your main tool for hit-and-run kiting, but also a block you'll often be forced to rely on.

### Tips

​- Keep your Voyage cards in a separate discard pile so both players can easily track your current movement and attack bonuses.
​- If you use the Mulligan rule and draw Voyage Home in your starting hand, shuffle it away if your opponent has discard effects. It is safer hidden in your deck.
​- Use Regroup and Commanding Impact to bait out the opponent's Feints before throwing a big Voyage attack.
​- The Porter’s only specific card, By Fortune and Fate, is very predictable. Treat the Porter as a 6-health meat shield to absorb hits or block paths; don't waste precious versatile cards keeping him alive.
​- Play your nasty early Voyages (like Apes or Whale) to force the opponent into a tough choice: take the effect early, or burn a valuable Feint that they will desperately need later.
​- Since none of the Voyages (except Voyage Home) have a boost value, you cannot use them to boost your maneuver. You must discard them at the end of your turn if you want them in the discard pile without playing them.
