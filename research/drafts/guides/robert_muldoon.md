## Robert Muldoon

**In short:** A ranged fighter who controls the map using traps and one-health workers. He wins a war of attrition by forcing the opponent to waste cards and actions navigating his defenses, but crumbles quickly if cornered in melee.

### Plan A: Control the map with traps

**Goal:** Restrict enemy movement and drain their health and cards before they reach you.
**How to do it:** At the start of your turn, you can place a trap in an unoccupied space in your zone. Opposing fighters who step on one take 1 damage, stop immediately, and you draw a card. Place traps in high-traffic choke points to build a wall between Muldoon and the opponent. You can attack with Rending Shot to force an opposing fighter up to 3 spaces away, or push them onto a trap to trigger the damage.
**Watch out for:** Do not place all your traps early. If you do, you lose the ability to deploy them when needed, and the forced card draws from popped traps will cause you to exhaust first. Keep at most two or three on the board. Note that fighters with move-through abilities (like Bigfoot or the Raptors) bypass the stop effect *if* you already have a fighter standing on that trap space, though they still take the damage and force you to draw.

### Plan B: Drain their deck with workers

**Goal:** Exhaust the opponent's resources by making them attack your sidekicks repeatedly.
**How to do it:** You start with three ranged InGen Workers, and the Call for Backup scheme can return all defeated workers to Muldoon's zone. Played twice, that forces the opponent to waste up to nine attacks just to clear your sidekicks. Send them forward to attack and keep Muldoon safely behind them. Workers can play any versatile or attack card not restricted to Muldoon. Attack with Second Shot and boost it with a 3-boost card like Tactical Advance to hit for 5 damage, or use Regroup to chip away safely. Melee fighters who rely on basic maneuver-and-attack patterns, such as Alice or Sinbad, will struggle to cut through this constant wall of bodies.
**Watch out for:** Be mindful of your zone. Call for Backup requires enough empty spaces in Muldoon's zone to fit all revived workers. Also, opponents can cancel worker attacks, so keep one Second Shot in hand as long as possible: the mere threat of a sidekick throwing a large attack forces them to use good defenses.

### Plan C: Punish positioning

**Goal:** Land large attacks when the opponent attempts to navigate the traps.
**How to do it:** They Should All Be Destroyed is a 4-value attack that adds 1 damage for each trap adjacent to the defender. You can choose the two scheme effects on Call for Backup to place up to 3 traps at once, surrounding an opponent to hit them with a massive attack on your second action. Shoot Her! is a 3-value attack that becomes a 5 if played as your first action.
**Watch out for:** Shoot Her! is extremely predictable. If you always use it as your opening move, opponents will easily block it. Keep them guessing by playing a blank attack or a low-value card first to bait out their Feint cards.

### How they lose

- He only has five strong, high-value defensive blocks (two copies of I've Hunted Most Things That Can Hunt You and three Leap Aways) and a low 14 health with no healing. Once his defenses run out, he dies fast.
- Feint is a weak defense for him. Canceling effects does not stop printed damage, and taking a few regular hits quickly drains his health.
- He is highly vulnerable to exhaustion. If the opponent ignores him and lets him continuously draw cards from his traps and Regroup effects, he will run out of cards first.
- Aggressive ranged fighters can shoot over the traps and sidekicks without ever triggering them, completely bypassing his zone control.

### Key cards

- **Call for Backup**: Almost always choose to return your defeated workers. For your second choice, placing traps is usually better than drawing 2 cards, which pushes you closer to exhaustion. You can even choose to place zero traps if you just want to pass the turn safely without drawing.
- **I've Hunted Most Things That Can Hunt You**: A 4-value defense that moves all of your fighters up to 5 spaces, even through enemies. It is his ultimate reset button when an enemy breaks through the worker wall, letting you completely reposition the team.
- **They Should All Be Destroyed**: His highest potential damage-dealer and a great card to bait out a Feint, since the printed 4 value still pushes damage through most blocks.
- **Remote Detonation**: A scheme that deals 1 damage to enemies adjacent to a trap in a worker's zone, then returns that trap. However, its best use is often as a blank card. Played when you have no workers or no traps on the board, it resolves with no effect, letting you safely waste a turn late in the game without drawing a card.

### Tips

- Never end your turn directly adjacent to a trap. If the opponent maneuvers onto it, they trigger it but will still be close enough to hit you with their second action.
- Stand on spaces that connect to multiple zones, like the lookout tower on the Raptor paddock map, to maximize the reach of your ranged attacks.
- Treat your traps as a limited resource. You only have eight for the entire game, and once they pop, they return to the box and are gone forever.
- You can play Tactical Advance on defense. It is only a 3-value block and the defending worker will likely die, but its after-combat effect lets you move your remaining workers up to 2 spaces to intercept an opponent's second action.
