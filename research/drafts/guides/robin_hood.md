## Robin Hood

**In short:** A fast, aggressive ranged fighter who avoids damage through constant movement rather than blocking. He surrounds himself with sidekicks to physically block the enemy and plays mind games with punishing attacks, while safely shooting from a distance.

### Plan A: Hit and run

**Goal:** Attack from safety and force the opponent to spend actions catching you.
**How to do it:** His special ability lets the attacking fighter move up to 2 spaces after a strike. Because Robin Hood is ranged, he can attack from the edge of a zone and immediately retreat. A melee opponent must spend their first action just maneuvering to get close, making it incredibly difficult for them to attack twice in a single turn. This constant kiting minimizes incoming attacks, making up for his low health pool. This tactic applies to his sidekicks, too: an Outlaw can run in for a melee hit and step back to safety.
**Watch out for:** His deck packs a lot of card draw, which means he burns through his deck quickly. You cannot just run away and drag out the game; you must keep up the offensive pressure to finish the fight before reaching exhaustion and taking fatigue damage.

### Plan B: The Highway Robbery trap

**Goal:** Punish the opponent whether they defend your sidekicks or not.
**How to do it:** The Outlaw-exclusive Highway Robbery cancels the opponent's card effects and ignores its defense value, guaranteeing 2 damage against any block except a cancel. When an Outlaw attacks, the defender faces a dilemma: waste a cancel on a 2-damage card, or play a regular defense and take the damage anyway. Once opponents start taking undefended hits to save their cancels, you can safely slip in a Regroup to win combat and draw two cards. You can also mix in Ambush to force a random discard and add its boost value to your hit for heavy, undefended damage.
**Watch out for:** Outlaws are melee, so they must maneuver close to set this trap. However, because opponents will hoard their cancels for the Outlaws, Robin's own attacks are much more likely to resolve without interference.

### Plan C: The Outlaw barricade

**Goal:** Protect Robin's small health pool.
**How to do it:** Robin Hood has a fragile 13 health, and his highest printed block is only a 3. To survive, position your four Outlaws around him to physically block paths and doorways, letting them take the hits. Defenders of Sherwood is a defense card that brings a defeated Outlaw back to any space in Robin's zone, effectively turning your four sidekicks into six over the course of the game. The versatile card Wily Fighting deals 1 damage to adjacent enemies after combat; use it defensively so its effect resolves before the attacker can use any movement to escape.
**Watch out for:** Any attack of 4 or higher guarantees a dead Outlaw. Accept that they will die, and often it is better to take a hit completely undefended to save your limited defensive cards for Robin himself.

### How they lose

- Big printed attacks (like Bigfoot's 6s) bleed right through his 3-value defenses, quickly depleting his low health.
- Effects that deal damage to adjacent fighters (like Bigfoot's Savagery) can clear out his Outlaw barricades efficiently.
- He draws so many cards that he often reaches exhaustion before the opponent, losing the game to his own deck.
- Opponents with 4-value attacks can guarantee Outlaw kills and reliably trigger their own "if you won the combat" effects, since Robin has no block cards higher than 3.

### Key cards

- **Highway Robbery**: The core of the Outlaw mind game. It ruins the opponent's defense card unless they play a cancel.
- **A Hunter's Eye**: A 5-value attack with no text. It hits hard, cannot be canceled, and heavily punishes opponents who guess wrong and waste a cancel on it.
- **Disarming Shot**: A 4-value attack that draws cards equal to the damage dealt. If played against an undefended 1-health sidekick, it deals 4 damage and draws 4 cards.
- **Defenders of Sherwood**: His most vital defense card. Save it strictly for when you have a defeated Outlaw in the discard pile to revive.
- **Steal From the Rich**: A scheme that draws a card and makes the opponent choose between discarding or letting you draw again. The sources disagree on its utility: one calls it a tricky tactical choice that forces mistakes, while others consider it a weak action better spent to boost a maneuver.
- **Piercing Shot**: A weak 2-value attack that draws two cards. It is an excellent safe play to poke the opponent and bait out their defense cards.

### Tips

- Save your faints (cancel cards) for Robin. Your Outlaws will usually die to a determined attack anyway, so don't waste a cancel trying to save them.
- A Hunter's Eye has a high boost value of 4. Don't hold it in your hand against fighters who use Ambush, or they will randomly discard it and add 4 to their own attack.
- Be careful when playing Snark or Wily Fighting to defend an Outlaw. If the opponent hits for 4 or more, the Outlaw is defeated and removed from the board before the "adjacent" after-combat effects can trigger. Use them on Robin Hood for more reliable results.
