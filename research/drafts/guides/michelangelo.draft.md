## Michelangelo

**In short:** An unpredictable, hyper-aggressive melee fighter with a hand limit of 3. He draws a card every time he attacks or schemes, letting him cycle through his deck quickly and chain action-gaining cards into massive combos.

### Plan A: Chain your actions

**Goal:** Play multiple cards in a single turn to overwhelm the opponent and build up a massive finishing attack.
**How to do it:** Most of your attacks are designed to keep your turn going. Boisterous beatdown (3 copies, value 2), Hi-yaaaaah!! (3 copies, value 4), and April's Hard-hitting investigation (3 copies, value 0) all grant you an action after combat. Because of your Pizza Party ability, you also draw a card every time you play an attack or a scheme, meaning these cards replace themselves in your hand. Keep attacking until you find a finisher. Cowabunga!! (3 copies, value 3) adds +1 to its value for every other card you played that turn, making it your best late-chain hit. Turtle power! (2 copies, value 3) is another strong finisher because you can boost it. Nunchaku (2 copies) is a scheme that adds +1 to all your attacks that turn, gives you an action, and draws a card, pushing your combo even higher.
**Watch out for:** Feints and other defensive cancels. If your opponent cancels the action gain on your first attack, your turn ends immediately. Play your weaker action-gaining attacks first to bait out their cancels before committing to a long chain.

### Plan B: Cycle the small hand

**Goal:** Manage your strict hand limit to find your combo pieces without overdrawing or wasting cards.
**How to do it:** Your starting and maximum hand size is 3, which means you must discard down to 3 at the end of every turn. Since you cannot hold many cards, you have to cycle them safely. Hi-yaaaaah!!, Guaranteed delivery (3 copies, value 3), and Shell insertion (2 copies) let you put a card from your hand on the bottom of your deck. This clears space in your hand before your mandatory card draw triggers, saving key cards for later in the game. If playing Guaranteed delivery leaves your hand entirely empty, it draws two cards after combat, instantly refueling you for the next turn.
**Watch out for:** The sources disagree on how much planning this requires. One argues you simply throw whatever you draw because the hand limit prevents real strategy, while another emphasizes carefully ordering your hand, tucking the right cards away, and setting up precise recursion loops.

### How they lose

- He hits exhaustion first. The mandatory draw on every attack and scheme becomes a severe liability when his deck is empty, dealing exhaustion damage every time he takes the offensive.
- Heavy aggression overwhelms him. With a maximum of three cards in hand, he simply does not have enough defensive options to survive multiple heavy attacks in a single turn.
- The opponent double maneuvers and runs away. He has to waste his few cards on boosts just to catch up, leaving him empty-handed when he finally engages.
- His action gains get canceled early in a turn, stopping his momentum before Cowabunga!! can grow.

### Key cards

- **Cowabunga!!**: his main finisher. A 3-value versatile that gets bigger with every card played previously in the turn.
- **Hi-yaaaaah!!**: a 4-value attack that does everything he wants. It deals 1 damage to an adjacent opposing fighter, tucks a card, gains an action, and draws a card.
- **Guaranteed delivery**: his only cancel. It requires tucking a card, which helps cycle, and it refuels your hand if it leaves it empty.
- **Hard-hitting investigation**: April's 0-value attack. It deals 1 guaranteed damage to the opposing fighter, gains an action, and draws a card.
- **Shell insertion**: April's versatile scheme. Choose the action gain and the recursion effect to shuffle used copies of Nunchaku or action-gaining attacks back into your deck.

### Tips

- Never double maneuver just to run away. Since attacking draws a card anyway, it is usually better to attack and force the opponent to discard a defense rather than just passing your turn to draw.
- Pin the opponent against the edge of the board using both Michelangelo and April so they cannot use movement effects to escape your combo turns.
- Use mind games with April. Opponents often take the hit from Hard-hitting investigation because it is a 0. You can surprise them by playing Guaranteed delivery instead to sneak 3 damage through.
- Don't hoard Heroes in a half shell (his only 5-value defense). With your tight hand limit, you need the space, so use it or tuck it.
- If you go first, push immediately. You can potentially win on turn one if you chain enough action gains together.
