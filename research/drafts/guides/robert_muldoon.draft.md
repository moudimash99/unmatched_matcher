## Robert Muldoon

**In short:** A ranged fighter who controls the map using traps and one-health workers. He wins a war of attrition by forcing the opponent to waste cards and actions navigating his defenses, but crumbles quickly if cornered in melee.

### Plan A: Control the map with traps

**Goal:** Restrict enemy movement and drain their health and cards before they reach you.
**How to do it:** At the start of your turn, you can place one of your 8 traps in an unoccupied space in your zone. When an opposing fighter steps on a trap, they take 1 damage, stop moving immediately, and you draw a card. Place traps in high-traffic choke points to build a wall between Muldoon and the opponent. You can use Rending Shot (4 copies, value 3) to move an opposing fighter up to 3 spaces into a trap, triggering the damage and stopping them.
**Watch out for:** Do not place all your traps early. If you do, you lose the ability to place them when you really need them, and the forced card draw from traps popping will cause you to exhaust first. Keep at most two or three on the board. Fighters with move-through effects (Bigfoot, Raptors) still take the damage and pop the trap, but their movement does not stop.

### Plan B: Drain their deck with workers

**Goal:** Exhaust the opponent's resources by making them attack your sidekicks repeatedly.
**How to do it:** You start with three InGen Workers, and Call for Backup can place all defeated workers back into Muldoon's zone. Played twice, that forces the opponent to waste up to 9 attacks just to clear your sidekicks. Send the workers forward to attack and keep Muldoon safely behind them. Workers can play any card that isn't restricted to Muldoon. Attack with Second Shot and boost it with a 3-value card like Tactical Advance, or use Regroup to replace your cards while chipping away at their defenses. Melee fighters who rely on basic maneuver-and-attack patterns (Alice, Sinbad) struggle to cut through this wall.
**Watch out for:** Be mindful of your zone. Since Call for Backup revives workers in Muldoon's zone, you need enough empty spaces around you to fit them all.

### Plan C: Punish positioning

**Goal:** Land large attacks when the opponent attempts to navigate the traps.
**How to do it:** They Should All Be Destroyed is a 4-value attack that adds 1 for each trap adjacent to the defender. You can use Call for Backup to place up to 3 traps at once, trapping an opponent and hitting them with a massive attack on your second action. Shoot Her! is a 3-value attack that becomes a 5 if played as your first action.
**Watch out for:** Shoot Her! is extremely predictable. If you always attack with it on your first action, opponents will just play their best defense. Keep them guessing by playing Regroup or a blank attack on your first action instead to bait out their Feints.

### How they lose

- He has only five strong defensive blocks (I've Hunted Most Things That Can Hunt You and Leap Away) and 14 health with no healing. Once those cards are gone, he dies fast.
- Feint is a weak defense for him. Canceling effects does not stop printed damage, and taking regular hits quickly drains his small health pool.
- He dies to exhaustion. If the opponent ignores him and lets him draw cards from his traps and Regroups, he will run out of cards first.
- Aggressive ranged fighters can shoot over the traps and sidekicks without triggering them.

### Key cards

- **Call for Backup**: a scheme with three options, and you choose two. Almost always choose to return your defeated workers. For the second option, placing traps is usually better than drawing 2 cards, unless you are desperate. You can choose to place zero traps if you just want to pass the turn safely.
- **I've Hunted Most Things That Can Hunt You**: a 4-value defense that moves all of your fighters up to 5 spaces, even through enemies. It is his best defense and the ultimate reset button when the enemy breaks through your wall.
- **Rending Shot**: a 3-value attack that moves the enemy up to 3 spaces. Push them into traps to deal damage and draw, or push them away to force them to waste maneuvers.
- **They Should All Be Destroyed**: his best damage-dealer and a great card to bait out a Feint, since a printed 4 still pushes damage through.
- **Remote Detonation**: a scheme that triggers a trap near a worker. The sources disagree on how to use it: one suggests using it to deal damage to enemies who refuse to step on a trap, while another argues its best use is as a blank card. Played when you have no workers or no traps in position, it resolves with no effect, safely wasting a turn late in the game without drawing a card.

### Tips

- Never end your turn directly adjacent to a trap. If the opponent maneuvers onto it, they trigger it but can still hit you with their second action.
- Stand on spaces that connect to multiple zones, like the lookout tower on the Raptor paddock map, to maximize the reach of your ranged attacks.
- Keep one Second Shot in hand as long as possible. The mere threat of a worker throwing a 5-value attack forces the opponent to play better defenses against them.
- Treat your traps as a limited resource. You only have 8 for the entire game, and once they return to the box, they are gone forever.
