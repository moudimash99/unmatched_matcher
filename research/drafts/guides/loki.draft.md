## Loki

**In short:** A tricky melee fighter who fills the opponent's hand with cards they don't want, then punishes them for holding a large hand. He thrives on mind games and forcing bad decisions.

### Plan A: Load their hand with tricks

**Goal:** Limit their options and make them pay a price to clear their hand.
**How to do it:** When you play a trick, it goes to their hand instead of your discard pile. Tricks have a boost value of 0, so the opponent cannot simply boost them away. If they discard a trick from their hand, it comes back to your hand or the top of your deck. Eventually, they have to play them, triggering the "Not Loki" effect: taking 2 damage from TRICK: Baldr's downfall or healing you for 3 with TRICK: FREYJA'S RESCUE.
**Watch out for:** When they play a trick as defense, it leaves their hand. If you attack with RAGNARÖK, that trick no longer counts for the +3 bonus. Always remember this timing when calculating your big attacks.

### Plan B: Punish a full hand

**Goal:** Turn their large hand size into massive attack values.
**How to do it:** Because you feed them cards, their hand size naturally grows. UNDERHANDED hits for 6 if their hand is larger than yours. Shapershifter gains +1 for every card they hold, and RAGNARÖK gains +3 for every trick. TRICK: SVADILFARI'S LURE is a perfect setup: it pulls them adjacent without spending an attack, leaving you with an action to launch one of these heavy hitters. Looking for trouble lets you look at their hand and force them to defend with the weakest card they hold.
**Watch out for:** If they play aggressively and keep their hand small, these cards do very little. Back away and force them to maneuver to draw cards.

### Plan C: Win through mind games

**Goal:** Bait out their best defenses and bypass them entirely.
**How to do it:** When they hold a trick, you know what it is because it has your card back. If you expect them to defend with a trick out of fear of RAGNARÖK, attack with GOD OF MISCHIEF. It ignores the trick's value entirely and pushes damage through. If they anticipate a big attack and throw a 4-value defense, attack with TRICK: FREYJA'S RESCUE instead. It swaps the combat values, meaning their 4 becomes yours and your 2 becomes theirs.
**Watch out for:** This relies heavily on guessing right. Mistakes are punished hard, leaving you attacking for 0 or 2 against a heavy block.

### How they lose

- Fighters with multiple sidekicks body-block him easily. With a move of 2, he struggles to reach the enemy hero through a crowd.
- His printed defenses top out at 3, leaving him vulnerable to heavy hitters.
- Opponents who quickly play the tricks they receive, accepting the damage or healing just to keep their hand clear of threats.
- Fighters whose cards change value during combat (like Momentous Shift) ruin his value-swapping math.

### Key cards

- **RAGNARÖK**: An uncancelable attack that adds 3 for each trick they hold. A value of 6 is a great target; don't wait all game trying to build a 9.
- **Looking for trouble**: A 4-value attack that lets you pick their defense. Use it to pull out a trick, or force a low-value card to guarantee damage.
- **Shapershifter**: A 0-value attack that adds 1 for every card in their hand, not just tricks. Alternatively, it gives an action. Take the value unless you desperately need the action.
- **TRICK: SVADILFARI'S LURE**: A scheme that acts as a pass and pulls them adjacent. Use it to set up a big hit on your second action.
- **GOD OF MISCHIEF**: A 2-value versatile. It acts as a Feint against normal cards, but against a trick, it completely ignores the value.

### Tips

- The sources completely disagree on his exhaustion game. Some argue his ability to place discarded tricks on his deck makes him an exhaustion master; others insist that feeding the opponent extra cards gives them too much fuel, making his fatigue game terrible.
- When an opponent discards your trick, choose where it goes based on your needs: the top of your deck to delay exhaustion, or your hand if you need immediate options.
- Play on smaller maps with tight centers. He needs to stay close to apply pressure, despite his move bonuses.
- Don't boost your maneuvers. Save your cards to maintain your own hand size, and let the opponent waste cards boosting to reach you.
- LAEVATEINN can grab a trick right back out of their hand. Use it to keep your best tricks in rotation.
