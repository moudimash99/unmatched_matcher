## Daredevil

**In short:** A tough solo melee fighter with only 22 cards. He keeps recycling his best cards into his deck with Devil of Hell's Kitchen, heals with Take A Knee, and blind boosts when his hand is small.

### Plan A: Keep the loop going
**Goal:** Never run out of cards, and wear the opponent down with healing and recycled cards.
**How to do it:** Devil of Hell's Kitchen (2 copies, value 4) puts itself and the last four cards you discarded back in your deck and shuffles, so a Devil that resolves comes back again. Play it early and often for the recycling, not only at the end for its value of 8. With an empty deck it's predictable, and every early Devil makes your deck bigger. Before you play it, look at the top four of your discard pile: you want Take A Knee, Breather, and Through Adversity or Feint, depending on the matchup. If a Devil is Feinted, get it back with Breather (3 copies). Two Devils plus three Breathers give you five tries against most opponents' three cancels.
**Watch out for:** One source argues the loop is fragile and Daredevil is weak because of it; most others find him strong once the loop is running. Losing a Devil to a discard effect or a blind boost hurts most when it was your last one.

### Plan B: Blind boost when the hand is small
**Goal:** Turn his 2- and 3-value cards into real threats on attack and defense.
**How to do it:** With 2 or fewer cards in hand (the card you just played doesn't count), you may blind boost: the top card of your deck adds its boost value. Most of his boosts are 2, so a 3-value block becomes about a 5. Man Without Fear can blind boost a second time. Feint is his only 1-boost card, so leaving the Feints out of your Devil shuffles makes every blind boost and Take A Knee worth at least 2. A late trick: with one card left in your deck, blind boost it away first, then Devil of Hell's Kitchen counts as 8.
**Watch out for:** Every blind boost removes a card from your deck, possibly a Devil or a Breather. Choose your hand size for the matchup: stay low to hit hard against fighters with weak defenses, and stay high against fighters who punish an empty hand.

### Plan C: Chip them while they hit you
**Goal:** Deal damage that doesn't depend on winning combats.
**How to do it:**
- Son of a Boxer (3 copies) deals 2 damage to an adjacent fighter when you lose the combat, so melee attackers pay for hitting you.
- Through Adversity (3 copies) moves him 4 spaces through enemies, dealing 1 to each fighter he passes. That's a cheap way to kill 1-health sidekicks like Outlaws or Harpies.

Recycled again and again, these add up to more damage than his attack values suggest.
**Watch out for:** Son of a Boxer only works on adjacent fighters, so it does little against ranged attackers.

### How they lose
- He has no card draw. Aggressive fighters with extra actions (Raptors, Tesla) catch him with no defenses left.
- Hand disruption (Black Widow, the Genie, Sherlock Holmes) takes his Devils and Breathers before he can use them.
- Lots of cancels (Bigfoot, Sherlock) Feint every Devil and stop the loop.
- He gets pinned. Through Adversity is his only reliable escape, because Grappling Hook can't move through enemies.
- His printed defenses top out at 3, so big hitters (Bigfoot, Achilles) get damage through every time.
- Ranged fighters with sidekicks keep hitting him while he chases.

### Key cards
- **Devil of Hell's Kitchen**: his whole game. It recycles five cards and can hit for 8 on an empty deck.
- **Take A Knee**: a 3-value versatile. After combat your deck's top card goes to the discard pile and you heal by its boost, usually 2. His best defense, and recycled healing.
- **Breather**: returns any non-scheme card from your discard pile. Use it on a Devil first.
- **Through Adversity**: escape a pin, clear small sidekicks, or pass without drawing.
- **Grappling Hook**: a 3-value versatile that moves him 2 after combat. Hit and step away, dodge a second attack, or close in on a ranged attacker.

### Tips
- Keep your discard pile in order; Devil of Hell's Kitchen depends on it.
- When Breather takes back a card, take the copy deepest in your discard pile. The ones near the top may come back with the next Devil anyway.
- Always keep one Through Adversity in hand, or still in your deck, against fighters with sidekicks.
- Against fighters whose sidekicks can't cancel (Little Red's Huntsman, Elektra's Hand), aim your Devils at the sidekicks.
- Larger maps with pockets and many connections suit him, because he's harder to pin there.
