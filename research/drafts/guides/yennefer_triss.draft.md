## Yennefer & Triss

**In short:** A ranged pair that forces you to pick your hero before the game begins. Both are glass cannons with very few reliable blocks. Yennefer plays a high-pressure game by boosting her attacks to punch through defenses, while Triss plays a longer, evasive game that punishes melee fighters with auto-damage.

### Plan A: Boost past their defenses (Yennefer)

**Goal:** Burn through the opponent's health and hand by boosting attacks after seeing their defense.
**How to do it:** Yennefer’s ability lets her boost her attack during the immediately phase, and the boost cannot be canceled. Because it happens immediately, you see the opponent's defensive card before you decide whether to spend a card to boost. If they play a low-value block or a cancel, add a boost to punish them. If they play a high-value block, save your cards.
To make her boosts hit harder, play Advisor to the king (3 copies). It is a scheme that gives your boosts +1 as an ongoing effect as long as you attack each turn. Play it as your first action, then attack to lock the effect in. When you want to boost, Echoing blast (3 copies, value 3) is your best target. If the boosted value wins the combat, Echoing blast returns to your hand, meaning you only spent the boost card rather than trading two of your cards for one of theirs.
**Watch out for:** Boosting rapidly accelerates your exhaustion clock. The sources disagree on whether you should boost Incinerate (value 7): one argues you should not boost it against average blocks and instead let them discard to ignore the damage, netting you a huge card advantage, while another highlights boosting it to push massive damage through cancels.

### Plan B: Kite and ping (Triss)

**Goal:** Survive aggressive melee fighters by dealing steady, unavoidable damage.
**How to do it:** If Triss is your hero, she deals 2 damage to an adjacent fighter after she plays a scheme. This gives you 10 potential auto-damage across your five scheme cards. Force melee opponents to spend actions moving adjacent to her, play a scheme to deal the damage, and then maneuver or attack to move away. Because Triss does not burn through her hand by boosting, she can play a longer value game. When the opponent's hand is down to three to five cards, hit them with Merigold's Hailstorm (2 copies, value 4). The opponent must choose the penalty, and at that hand size, taking 3 damage is often less devastating than discarding two crucial cards.
**Watch out for:** Triss's ability hits *any* adjacent fighter. If Yennefer is the only fighter next to Triss when you play a scheme, Yennefer takes the damage. Triss also struggles against ranged opponents, who can attack her without ever triggering the adjacency requirement.

### Plan C: Bait and reposition

**Goal:** Attack constantly to force the opponent to waste defenses and keep them at a distance.
**How to do it:** Because their printed blocks are weak, staying safe means keeping the opponent's hand small and their fighters far away. Portal to anywhere (3 copies) is a 1-value attack that places a fighter anywhere and gains an action. Attack with it to draw out a block, place the opposing fighter far away, and use the gained action to maneuver further away. Alternatively, use the action to combo into a heavy attack like Incinerate.
Your versatile cards also perform much better on attack than on defense. Telepathy (3 copies, value 3) makes the opponent choose between discarding a card or having their card's effects canceled. Ball lightning (3 copies, value 3) forces a discard if you win, or mutual discards if you don't. Launching these as attacks forces the opponent into bad choices and protects your fragile fighters.
**Watch out for:** Using your versatile cards to attack leaves you with very few cards to play on defense.

### How they lose

​- They have terrible defenses. Magical barrier is their only 4-value block. Heavy hitters push damage through their 2s and 3s easily.
​- Aggressive fighters overwhelm them before Yennefer can set up her ongoing scheme or Triss can draw hers.
​- Yennefer runs out of cards and dies to the exhaustion clock because she boosted too often and traded poorly against high blocks.
​- Opponents focus down the 6-health sidekick early, removing valuable body blocks and specific cards (like Merigold's Hailstorm for Triss).

### Key cards

​- **Incinerate**: Yennefer's 7-value attack. The opponent may discard 2 cards to ignore the value. Even unboosted, the threat of it forces the opponent to keep cards in hand.
​- **Merigold's Hailstorm**: Triss's 4-value attack. Forces the opponent to choose between taking 3 damage, discarding 2 cards, or discarding 4 from their deck.
​- **Magical barrier**: a 4-value defense that heals 1 immediately. Your only reliable block. It has a boost of 3, but you usually cannot afford to boost with it.
​- **Advisor to the king**: an ongoing scheme that heals, draws, and adds +1 to your boosts as long as you attack. It is Yennefer's most important setup card, but provides almost no benefit to Triss.
​- **Portal to anywhere**: a 1-value attack that gains an action. Your premier bait card to waste opponent defenses and reposition the board.

### Tips

​- If Yennefer is the hero, keep Triss far away to draw enemy attacks, or attack with Triss using Portal to anywhere to pull the enemy into Yennefer's range.
​- Paralyzing fetters locks the opponent's card to its printed value. Use it to stop cards that bump their values during combat, or to shut down uncancelable effects (like Winter Soldier's attacks).
​- Save Lodge of sorceresses for when hands are very full to force the opponent to overdraw, or very empty so they are less likely to reveal a high boost value, minimizing your self-damage.
​- Avoid playing Ball lightning on defense. You will likely lose the combat and be forced to discard.
​- Triss excels on maps with item tokens, as using a scheme token counts as playing a scheme and triggers her auto-damage.
