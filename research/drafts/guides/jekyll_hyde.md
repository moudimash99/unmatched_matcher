## Jekyll & Hyde

**In short:** A form-switching solo fighter who plays defensively to draw cards as Dr. Jekyll, then transforms into Mr. Hyde to unleash massive attacks. Since Mr. Hyde hurts himself when maneuvering, he relies on card effects and positioning to let the opponent come to him.

### Plan A: Build a massive Forever Hyde

**Goal:** Finish the game with one massive, heavily boosted attack.
**How to do it:** Forever Hyde (2 copies) is a 5-value attack that allows you to discard Dr. Jekyll cards during combat, adding 2 to the attack's value for each one. To fuel this win condition, stay in Dr. Jekyll's form for roughly 75% of the match. Stockpile cards using Scientific Method, a 2-value defense that draws cards equal to the damage you absorb. Because you draw after combat on the opponent's turn, this can safely push your hand size well past the 7-card limit, setting up a gigantic attack for your next turn. Keep your weakest Jekyll cards—specifically Distracted Triage and Succumb to Compulsion—strictly to use as discard fodder.
**Watch out for:** Feints, which cancel the card's effect and leave the attack at a flat 5. Additionally, discarding too many defensive cards too early will leave you helpless later in the match, so avoid throwing away your best blocks.

### Plan B: Move Hyde without maneuvering

**Goal:** Engage the opponent as Mr. Hyde without suffering his maneuver penalty.
**How to do it:** Mr. Hyde takes 1 damage every time he maneuvers. To bypass this, force the opponent to approach you instead; if they maneuver and attack, they will end their turn adjacent to your fighter. You can then transform into Hyde at the start of your turn and attack them twice. If you must be the one to close the gap, use Pure Evil, a scheme that places Hyde anywhere in his current zone and deals 2 damage to all adjacent fighters. Alternatively, defend an incoming attack as Jekyll using With Haste! to move up to 4 spaces toward the opponent, putting you in prime position to shift to Hyde on your turn.
**Watch out for:** Pure Evil only lets you place your fighter within their current zone. Opponents paying close attention to board areas can stand just out of reach to deny the effect entirely. If you are ever forced to maneuver as Hyde, it is highly recommended to discard a card to boost your movement so you can cross the gap in a single action.

### Plan C: Don't get caught defending as Hyde

**Goal:** Revert to Dr. Jekyll before the opponent can retaliate.
**How to do it:** Mr. Hyde has no dedicated defense cards. If you end your turn next to the opponent while in his form, they will likely punish you. Use Recoiling Blow as your last action: it is an attack that hits for 5, places you anywhere in your zone, and transforms you back to Jekyll. If you do get stuck as Hyde on the opponent's turn, Madness Relents is your best defensive option. It is a versatile card that blocks for 4 and transforms you back into Jekyll, protecting you from their second action.
**Watch out for:** Opponents know that Recoiling Blow is your primary escape route and will regularly Feint it. While it will still punch through a Feint for 3 damage, you will be trapped as Hyde. Enemy cards that force you to move after combat can also strand Hyde far away from the action, requiring a painful maneuver on your following turn.

### How they lose

- Aggressive opponents relentlessly attack Jekyll to force him into playing his defense cards early, starving Forever Hyde of fuel.
- They get stranded as Hyde with no transformation cards in hand, taking heavy damage from sub-optimal blocks or their own maneuver penalty.
- Their turns become highly predictable. Jekyll's attacks are weak and Hyde's are dangerous, telling opponents exactly when to take the hit and when to spend their Feints and strong blocks.
- Their low movement values and maneuver penalty make it incredibly painful to chase down ranged fighters and kiters.
- They push their luck drawing cards with Calming Research and are forced to bury their best attacks at the bottom of the deck.

### Key cards

- **Forever Hyde**: The deck's primary win condition that scales to massive numbers based on your discarded Jekyll cards.
- **Duality of Man**: A versatile card that becomes an incredible 6-value block for Jekyll or a 6-value attack for Hyde. Never use it for its base value of 3.
- **Recoiling Blow**: A strong Hyde attack that simultaneously resets your board position and your personality form.
- **Scientific Method**: A Jekyll defense that turns absorbed damage into drawn cards. Use it against heavy hits to completely fill your hand.
- **Calming Research**: A Jekyll scheme that heals 2 health and lets you look at up to 3 cards. You keep one, but the rest go to the bottom of the deck. Opt to draw just 1 to avoid burying key cards, or choose to draw 0 to safely stall a turn when your hand is already full.
- **With Haste!**: A Jekyll defense that moves him up to 4 spaces. Essential for repositioning near the enemy without taking damage.

### Tips

- When With Haste! is played on defense, its movement resolves before the attacker's after-combat effects. This allows you to step away and break the attack chains of aggressive fighters like Bruce Lee.
- Save your Skirmish cards exclusively to defend as Hyde. He desperately needs the 4-value versatile blocks, while Jekyll has better defensive options.
- Use Pure Evil to drop adjacent to the opponent, then attack with a weaker card like Skirmish. Since they expect a massive Forever Hyde, this often baits out a Feint.
- Strange Case deals damage equal to the boost value of your top card, but reveals that card to the opponent. Avoid maneuvering and taking damage as Hyde just to play this, since the randomly revealed card might only hit for 1.
- If you have an early health lead, aggressively take damage on Scientific Method to quickly build a massive hand of cards.
- Do not bother attacking as Dr. Jekyll. His attacks cap out at a value of 3, so save your actions to draw cards or position yourself for Mr. Hyde.
