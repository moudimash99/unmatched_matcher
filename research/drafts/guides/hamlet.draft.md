## Hamlet

**In short:** A flexible, high-pressure fighter who shifts between extreme card draw and massive damage bursts. He uses his sidekick as a health battery and requires careful deck management to avoid destroying himself through exhaustion or self-damage.

### Plan A: Hit and run with NOT TO BE

**Goal:** Build a large hand safely, then unleash massive attacks in a single turn.
**How to do it:** Use his TO BE ability to draw extra cards when maneuvering, positioning yourself and building a hand of 6 or more cards. Once you have the right cards, declare NOT TO BE at the start of your turn to give Hamlet's attacks a +2 value.

During these burst turns, *Cruel To Be Kind* (a 2-value attack) becomes a 4, and you may boost it. With a 4-value boost card, it swings for 8. *The Play's The Thing* becomes a 5-value attack that forces a random discard; playing it on attack rather than defense makes it much more likely you will win the combat and trigger its healing effect. If the opponent tries to run, *The Readiness Is All* pins them to their space so they cannot escape your second attack. *The Ghost* can teleport Hamlet anywhere in his zone and grant an action, letting you close the distance without maneuvering and launch a double attack.
**Watch out for:** The 2 damage from NOT TO BE. Sources disagree on who should take it: most players use Rosencrantz & Guildenstern exclusively as a health battery since they can absorb three uses of the ability, while one prefers using Hamlet's health early to keep the sidekick alive for body blocking.

### Plan B: Play the exhaustion game

**Goal:** Control your deck size and outlast opponents who refuse to approach.
**How to do it:** Hamlet's TO BE ability forces him to draw heavily, which can quickly lead to exhaustion if you double-maneuver. *Method In The Madness* fixes this. You can maneuver (drawing two cards), play the scheme, place your unwanted cards on the bottom of your deck, choose to draw none, and gain an action to maneuver again. This keeps your hand size manageable, stalls the exhaustion clock, and lets you stack the bottom of your deck in a known order for the late game.

If an opponent hangs back to let you deck yourself out, use *Method In The Madness* and *The Ghost* as turn skips. You can maintain a deck advantage while using *The Readiness Is All* and *To Sleep, Perchance To Dream* to play keep-away.
**Watch out for:** Bad draw luck. If you do not find *Method In The Madness* early, you are forced into making aggressive NOT TO BE plays before you drain your own deck.

### Plan C: Punish versatile cards

**Goal:** Exploit fighters who rely on versatile cards for their defense.
**How to do it:** Several of Hamlet's cards specifically counter versatile cards, making him dangerous against fighters who lack dedicated defense cards (like Shakespeare, Sinbad, or Tesla). *Uncertain Doom* ignores the value of versatile cards entirely, guaranteeing its damage goes through. On defense, *Nothing Either Good Or Bad* automatically deals 2 damage to an opponent attacking with a versatile card, allowing you to chip away at their health even when it fails to block the attack fully.
**Watch out for:** Opponents who know your deck and purposefully save their dedicated defense cards for your *Uncertain Doom* attacks.

### How they lose

**Goal:**
​- His printed defense values are low. Fighters with big printed attacks (Bigfoot, Luke Cage) easily overwhelm his 2-value blocks and burst him down.
​- He draws through his deck too fast. If he fails to manage his hand with his schemes, he kills himself with exhaustion damage.
​- Feints ruin his sustain. He relies heavily on the after-combat healing of *The Play's The Thing* and *To Sleep, Perchance To Dream*.
​- Taking too much self-damage from NOT TO BE without successfully landing his healing cards leaves him critically low on health.

### Key cards

​- **Method In The Madness**: His most important tool for survival. It prevents overdrawing, recycles your deck, and lets you sculpt the late game.
​- **Cruel To Be Kind**: His biggest attack. With NOT TO BE and a 4-value boost, it hits for 8.
​- **The Rest Is Silence**: A 2-value defense that ends the turn. Because it resolves before the opponent's after-combat effects, it halts momentum and stops fighters who trigger abilities after an attack.
​- **Maddening Insight**: A 0-value defense whose value equals your hand size. Because TO BE keeps your hand full, this is often his strongest block.
​- **The Play's The Thing**: A 3-value versatile that forces a random discard. While it can be used in a pinch on defense, it is best used as a NOT TO BE attack to guarantee the heal.

### Tips

​- Rosencrantz & Guildenstern do not have any cards of their own. They can only play "Any" cards. Use them to harass the opponent with *To Sleep, Perchance To Dream* so that if they lose the combat, Hamlet still recovers the health.
​- *Blood Will Have Blood* deals 1 damage if Hamlet took damage *this turn*. If you assign the 2 damage from NOT TO BE to Hamlet, this card's effect will automatically trigger.
​- Pay attention to the opponent's discard pile. If they still have Feints available (Robin Hood, for example), attack with your healing cards instead of defending with them, so you dictate when the combat happens.
​- *Outrageous Fortune* requires you to discard the top card of your deck, not draw it. If your deck is empty, you do not take exhaustion damage for this effect.
