## Beowulf

**In short:** A bulky melee fighter who treats damage as a resource. He absorbs hits to generate Rage, which he then spends on uncancelable attacks, emergency blocks, and healing, while using his many non-drawing schemes to win the exhaustion game.

### Plan A: Manage the Rage economy

**Goal:** Keep enough Rage to threaten powerful effects without wasting the free tokens you gain from taking damage.
**How to do it:** Beowulf starts with 1 Rage and gains 1 whenever he takes damage, up to a maximum of 3. Try to end your turn with 1 or 2 Rage rather than a full 3. This forces the opponent into a difficult choice: attack and give you your final resource, or leave you alone. To fuel this, do not over-block. It is often better to intentionally take 1 or 2 damage on defense to generate Rage safely. When you attack with Epic Poem (value 2, immediately gain 1 Rage), plan it so you have exactly two Rage before combat. The free token pushes you to the maximum and makes the card a 5, without spilling over the limit.
**Watch out for:** Spending Rage randomly. Save it for high-impact moments. If your opponent refuses to hit you and you desperately need Rage for your next turn, Vigor and Courage forces them to discard a random card and gives you Rage equal to its boost.

### Plan B: Outlast them with exhaustion

**Goal:** Conserve your deck and force the opponent to burn their cards to reach you.
**How to do it:** Beowulf has a flexible game plan that excels at fatigue. He has seven scheme cards that do not force you to draw, giving him plenty of passes to stall the exhaustion clock. Because his movement is a sluggish 2, do not discard cards to boost your movement; force the opponent to come to you instead. Let Wiglaf use his 9 health to body block and absorb hits. When you play Golden Drinking Horn (2 copies, scheme), prioritize recovering 2 health or moving 4 spaces, and avoid the card draw option entirely so your deck stays thick.
**Watch out for:** Fatal Struggle. If you win the combat with this 4-value attack, you must draw two cards, which accelerates your own exhaustion. Play it when the opponent's hand is full so that if you lose, their forced draw makes them discard, or target a weak sidekick to ensure you win only when you actually want the cards.

### Plan C: Unleash uncancelable attacks

**Goal:** Land massive damage that ignores their defensive tricks.
**How to do it:** The Ancient Heirloom (2 copies, value 3) is your best attack because its effects cannot be canceled by a Feint. You can spend 2 Rage to change its base value to 5, and spend 1 Rage to boost it. If you cannot afford both, prioritize spending 1 Rage to boost it with a high-value card like No Contest Expecteth. That brings the attack to a 6 for half the Rage cost.
**Watch out for:** Sherlock Holmes. His Elementary defense completely ignores the attack value rather than canceling effects. Spending your Rage to boost The Ancient Heirloom against him still results in zero damage.

### How they lose

- He has very few dedicated defense cards. Outside of his signature block, he relies heavily on versatile cards to defend.
- Opponents who strike with single massive attacks bypass his bulk. He wants to take tiny hits to build Rage safely; large hits deplete his 17 health too fast.
- The opponent simply refuses to attack him when his Rage is low, starving him of his main resource.

### Key cards

- **The Equal of Grendel**: a 3-value defense. For 2 Rage, it immediately deals the printed value of the opponent's card back to them. Holding 2 Rage makes attackers hesitant to throw their heaviest hits, like Bigfoot's Larger Than Life.
- **The Ancient Heirloom**: his primary damage dealer. Uncancelable, and it scales heavily when you have the Rage to alter its value and boost it simultaneously.
- **The War-King**: a 1-value versatile. You spend any amount of Rage to add +2 per token. Because you choose how much to spend during combat, it works as a perfectly adaptable emergency defense to match an incoming attack.
- **Golden Drinking Horn**: a scheme that lets you spend Rage for different effects (draw, move, heal). You must pick a different effect for each Rage spent; you cannot choose to heal 6.
- **Remnant of Valor**: a Wiglaf scheme that deals 1 damage to adjacent fighters. Hitting Beowulf with this deals minimal damage, but cleanly generates a Rage and refunds your action.

### Tips

- Wiglaf has no defense cards of his own. Save your copies of Skirmish to protect him.
- Do not waste 3 Rage on No Contest Expecteth to defeat a sidekick, even a strong one like Merlin or Patroclus. It is almost always better saved as a 3-boost for The Ancient Heirloom.
- Exploit enemy abilities that deal automatic chip damage, such as Medusa's end-of-turn ability, by standing in her zone to gain free Rage.
- Use Hot for the Battle to strike with Wiglaf, then instantly swap spaces to pull him out of danger and put Beowulf on the frontline.
