## Sun Wukong

**In short:** A high-health melee fighter who hides behind a wall of clones. His entire deck can be played by his sidekicks, letting him attack risk-free and force the opponent into brutal guessing games.

### Plan A: Build a wall of clones

**Goal:** Drain the opponent's resources by making them fight your disposable sidekicks instead of you.
**How to do it:** At the start of your turn, take 1 damage to summon a clone. Keep Wukong surrounded or out of reach. All 30 cards in your deck are "Any" cards, meaning your clones attack and defend with the full strength of the hero. When an opponent uses an action and an attack card to destroy a clone, they spend far more than the 1 health it cost you to make it. If you need a clone without paying health, defend with Sly Monkey to drop a clone and move Wukong away. To really frustrate the opponent, occasionally defend a clone with Tortoise Form (a 5-value defense), which forces them to waste a high boost card if they want the kill.
**Watch out for:** Don't summon blindly if your health gets low, and do not group your fighters together against enemies with area of effect damage. Wily Fighting or fighters like the T-Rex can wipe out multiple clones at once for very little cost.

### Plan B: The combat mind game

**Goal:** Punish the opponent no matter what they do, draining their hand with bad trades.
**How to do it:** His attacks create terrible choices. If they do not block, Ox Form hits for a printed 7. If they do block, Ruyi Jingo Bang triggers a 4-value bonus attack. To survive Ox Form, they have to boost their defense, meaning they spend two cards to survive one attack. Taunting Laughter forces them to discard a card or you ignore their defense value. All of this is devastating when they have a small hand size. Use Fiery Eyes That See to look at their hand and bury their best card at the bottom of their deck. Then, use Infinite Strikes to gain an action and throw the exact attack they cannot handle.
**Watch out for:** Losing the threat. If you play both copies of Ox Form early, the opponent knows no more 7s are coming and can safely use weak blocks on your other attacks. Keep at least one big threat in hand to maintain the pressure.

### Plan C: Loop the forms

**Goal:** Play your strongest cards up to five times.
**How to do it:** 72 Transformations (3 copies) returns Ox Form, Tortoise Form, or Phoenix Form from your discard pile. If you return Phoenix Form while three clones are out, you can heal up to 12 health over the game, easily recovering the damage spent on your ability. If you return Ox Form, you can throw five 7-value attacks, which exhausts the opponent's blocks quickly. The choice depends on the game state: loop Phoenix Form to outlast them in exhaustion, or loop Ox Form to crush them.
**Watch out for:** 72 Transformations is useless if it gets canceled. Instead of attacking with it, use it to defend a clone against a weak attack. Even if the clone dies, the effect safely goes off, or it baits the opponent into wasting a valuable cancel on a sidekick.

### How they lose

- He has no card draw outside of a scheme that replaces itself, and zero cancels in his deck. This leaves him vulnerable to powerful effects and auto-damage.
- Fighters with ping damage, discard effects, and move-through abilities (Daredevil, Ghost Rider, Yennenga) clear his clones effortlessly and bypass his wall.
- He relies heavily on Phoenix Form to recover his health, but it is a single copy. If it sits at the bottom of the deck, he might drain his own health before drawing it.
- His best defenses (Golden Chain Mail, Bewilderment) have a base value of 4 and 0. If an opponent cancels them, the damage prevention fails and he takes massive hits.

### Key cards

- **Ox Form**: a 7-value attack. The effect actually helps the opponent by letting them boost, but they have to spend a second card to do it.
- **Ruyi Jingo Bang**: a 0-value attack that hits for 4 if they defend. The ultimate punishment for blocking.
- **Golden Chain Mail**: prevents all combat damage and deals it back to the attacker. Save this for Wukong, as it perfectly stops massive hits unless canceled.
- **Fiery Eyes That See**: look at their hand and bury a card at the bottom of their deck. Because most fighters cannot shuffle their decks, that card is gone for the whole game.
- **Phoenix Form**: heals 1 health for every clone on the board. A crucial card to survive your own clone-summoning damage.
- **72 Transformations**: returns a form card to your hand. Crucial for extending your health pool or damage output.

### Tips

- When going first, summon a clone immediately. This effectively starts the game with a two-clone advantage before the opponent can safely engage.
- Infinite Strikes counts friendly fighters adjacent to the target. In a 2v2 game, it counts your teammate's fighters too.
- Play Bewilderment defensively on Wukong to prevent all damage and teleport to safety, escaping pins so you have empty spaces to summon more clones.
- Use Wily Fighting on defense. If you attack with it, the opponent might move away after combat, completely dodging the area damage.
