## William Shakespeare

**In short:** A complex, aggressive melee fighter who strings cards together to form 10-syllable lines. He relies on powerful unblockable effects and extra actions, but with low health and weak defenses, he heavily depends on his three Actors to absorb damage.

### Plan A: Threaten extra actions

**Goal:** Complete a line with My Kingdom For a Horse to take back-to-back turns.
**How to do it:** His special ability, Iambic Pentameter, adds every card you play in combat to your line. When the line hits exactly 10 syllables, the last card's completion effect triggers. *My Kingdom For a Horse* (3 copies, value 5) is a 6-syllable card that grants 2 extra actions on completion. To set this up, play a 4-syllable card on your first action, putting you at exactly 4 syllables. Your best setup is *All Are Punished* (2 copies, value 4), a strong attack. On your second action, attack with *My Kingdom For a Horse*. Not only is it a 5-value attack, but you immediately gain 2 actions to retreat, maneuver, or attack again. Every time your line sits at 4 syllables, the opponent has to fear this combo and may waste a vital defense card.
**Watch out for:** Because discards outside of combat do not add to your line, you cannot adjust your syllable count by boosting. Opponents can track your syllables and will anticipate the 6-syllable drop.

### Plan B: The Horror finisher

**Goal:** Deal guaranteed damage that ignores blocks and Feints.
**How to do it:** *Horror* is a 4-value attack, and you have five copies of it. Its value increases by +1 for each other *Horror* in your line, but its real threat is the completion effect: it deals 1 damage to the opposing fighter for each other card in your line. Because *Horror* is a 2-syllable card, it is easy to fit at the end of an 8-syllable line. Do not wait for a perfect line of five *Horrors*. Playing it as the third or fourth card in a mixed line still gives you 2 or 3 unblockable damage, turning a 4-value attack into a massive hit. Since completion effects trigger after combat and resolve directly from the special ability, a Feint does not stop this damage.
**Watch out for:** Pushing for maximum value by playing four *Horrors* in a row is too risky and means you are missing out on completing other lines.

### Plan C: Survive and sustain

**Goal:** Keep a 13-health hero alive and maintain card advantage.
**How to do it:** Shakespeare lacks true defense cards and has very few blocks above a 3. He needs his Actors to take hits for him. Block with *Such Sweet Sorrow* (Actor only, 2 copies) to force opponents to waste big attacks on a sidekick, then move up to 2 spaces away to dodge a second attack. If the Actors die, *Once More Unto The Breach* (2 copies, value 3) returns a defeated Actor to his zone, and revives all of them if it completes a line. For Shakespeare himself, *Alas* (2 copies, value 2) is critical. It heals 1 if you lose the combat, but completing a line with it draws 2 cards and heals 2. Since he lacks standard schemes to pass with, completing *Alas* is his best engine for health and card advantage.
**Watch out for:** *All Are Punished* deals 1 damage to each other fighter in the zone, which will easily kill your own Actors if they are standing too close.

### How they lose

- Big printed attacks easily break through his 2- and 3-value defenses. Once his Actors fall, Shakespeare's 13 health drops fast.
- Opponents manipulate his line. They attack him when he is at 4 syllables to force him to play a block, ruining his setup for *My Kingdom For a Horse*.
- He gets starved of cards. *Alas* and *Revise* are his only reliable ways to draw, and he has no non-combat cards to stall the tempo.
- Fighters who can ignore body blocks (like Bullseye) or fighters with constant auto-damage run him out of health before his combos assemble.

### Key cards

- **My Kingdom For a Horse**: a 5-value attack that grants 2 actions on completion. The core of his aggressive pressure.
- **Horror**: a 4-value attack with 5 copies in the deck. Great for unblockable damage on completion.
- **Alas**: a 2-value versatile that heals 1 if you lose the combat. On completion, it draws 2 cards and heals 2. His most crucial card for health and card advantage.
- **Deceive**: a 2-value versatile that cancels effects. On completion, it returns to your hand, giving you a reusable Feint.
- **Once More Unto The Breach**: a 3-value versatile that revives one Actor. Try to use it on defense to revive all of them when the line completes.
- **Et Tu, Brute?**: a 3-value versatile whose value increases for each friendly fighter adjacent to the target. Often his highest-value block.
- **Again**: a 3-value versatile for the Actors. It allows you to return the first card of your line to your hand, letting you fix a broken syllable count or recur a valuable card.

### Tips

- Completion effects happen even on the opponent's turn. Use this to trigger *Once More Unto The Breach* or *Deceive* on defense.
- Use *Deceive* defensively to complete a line, returning it to your hand so you still hold a cancel for their next attack.
- The sources disagree on *The Ides of March*: one recommends completing lines with it to deal unblockable damage and bait strong defenses, while another calls it the worst card in the deck because its base value of 2 with no effect is too weak to risk playing.
- Smaller maps with pockets (like Raptor Paddock or Globe Theatre) suit him best, allowing him to attack without boosting and safely hide behind his Actors.
