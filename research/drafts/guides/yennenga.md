## Yennenga

**In short:** A tanky double-ranged fighter who uses her sidekicks to absorb damage, then overwhelms the opponent with action-heavy combo turns. She punishes poor positioning but struggles against heavy card effects.

### Plan A: Maximize the health pool

**Goal:** Keep Yennenga safe and outlast the opponent's damage.
**How to do it:** Yennenga's ability lets her assign any amount of damage she would take to one or more Archers in her zone. Keep the Archers close to her so they can absorb hits. Since each Archer has 2 health, you can assign them 1 damage and keep them alive as a defensive wall. Play the scheme One With The Land to heal Yennenga and each Archer for 1 health. With both Archers injured but alive, that recovers 3 total health. If an Archer dies, you have tools to bring them back. The attack Surprise Volley can return a defeated Archer to the opposing fighter's zone. The defense card Shield Formation forces the opponent to either discard a card or let you revive an Archer in Yennenga's zone.
**Watch out for:** Area-of-effect attacks. Her ability cannot assign more damage to an Archer than its remaining health, and you cannot make damage disappear. If an attack deals damage to all fighters in a zone simultaneously, shifting damage accomplishes nothing, and Yennenga still takes the hit.

### Plan B: The action combo

**Goal:** Run the opponent out of block cards with multiple attacks in a single turn.
**How to do it:** Master of the Hunt is a scheme that gives you 2 actions, leaving you with three actions for the turn. Use them to launch a barrage of attacks. Surprise Volley gives you a choice: revive an Archer or gain an action. Taking the action keeps the chain going. Follow up with Rain of Arrows, an attack that triggers a bonus strike after combat called VOLLEY. By forcing the opponent to defend four or five attacks in one turn, you drain their card advantage and land undefended hits.
**Watch out for:** Playing Master of the Hunt as your first action telegraphs your combo. Attack first, see how they block, and play the scheme later in the turn. You also need a large hand size to pull this off without exhausting your options.

### Plan C: Punish their positioning

**Goal:** Exploit enemy placement and clear sidekicks without wasting attacks.
**How to do it:** Stallion Charge moves Yennenga up to 5 spaces through opposing fighters, dealing 1 damage to each one she passes. Use it to instantly clear 1-health sidekicks, like Wukong's clones or Robin Hood's Outlaws, just by moving. On defense, Jaws of the Beast increases its printed value by +1 for each zone the opposing fighter is in. Against ranged fighters who stand in multi-zone spaces to see the whole board, this card reliably hits 5 or 6. To disrupt enemy placement, the Archer versatile card Pin the Prey moves the opponent up to 4 spaces into a bad position and forces them to discard a card.
**Watch out for:** Stallion Charge only damages each fighter once per turn, even if you move back and forth over them. Smart opponents will spread their sidekicks out so you cannot hit them all with one move.

### How they lose

- She has no Feints and no cards that cancel or ignore enemy effects. Fighters who rely on heavy combat abilities (like Sinbad or Bruce Lee) hit her completely unchecked.
- She lacks scheme passes, which makes her struggle against opponents playing for the exhaustion clock.
- Opponents who efficiently kill her Archers first remove her damage distribution, leaving her as a standard 15-health hero.
- Melee fighters who separate her from her Archers or force her out of their zone turn off her defensive ability entirely.

### Key cards

- **Rain of Arrows**: Generates massive card advantage if the opponent tries to block both the initial hit and the bonus attack.
- **Stallion Charge**: Her best escape tool and a guaranteed way to clear out small sidekicks. Play it on defense to reposition on their turn.
- **Jaws of the Beast**: A scaling versatile card. Usually her strongest defense against big attacks if you can force the opponent to strike from a multi-zone space.
- **Surprise Volley**: Acts as either an Archer revive or an action-gaining combo extender.
- **Master of the Hunt**: The engine for her combo turns. Play it when the opponent is low on cards.
- **One With The Land**: Her only healing and card draw. Moving each fighter 2 spaces helps reset her formation.

### Tips

- Keep your hand size high. Yennenga's strength comes from throwing multiple cards in a single turn.
- When playing Shield Formation, expect the opponent to discard a card rather than let you revive an Archer. However, if both of your Archers are currently alive, the opponent will not have to discard a card and the effect is wasted entirely.
- Mulligan for the high-value versatile card Skirmish if you go second against fighters with strong first-turn combos, like Tesla. You should also mulligan for Stallion Charge if the opponent relies on swarms of 1-health sidekicks.
- Be careful when reviving an Archer with Surprise Volley against an opponent with damage-dealing defensive effects (like Little Red). Because the revived Archer immediately becomes the attacker, they can be instantly killed by the combat outcome.
- Point Blank deals 2 damage if the opposing fighter is adjacent. It is risky to use on defense, but serves as a solid counter against melee attackers pressing you if you have Archers nearby to absorb the combat damage.
- The sources disagree on Divide and Conquer: one views its 4-value effect as a reason to send Archers out of Yennenga's zone, while another argues the lost defensive ability makes it the worst card in her deck.
