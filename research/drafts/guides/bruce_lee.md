## Bruce Lee

**In short:** A fast solo melee fighter who chains multiple attacks together in a single turn. He ducks away after attacking to limit return strikes, but relies on low-value blocks and has no way to heal.

### Plan A: Chain attacks together

**Goal:** Play a long sequence of attacks to drain the opponent's defenses and push damage through.
**How to do it:** His six Jeet Kune Do cards are all 3-value attacks that give you an action after combat. Because they replace the action spent to play them, you can string several together in one turn. Start a chain with Jeet Kune Do: Wrist Lock to draw out a Feint, or Jeet Kune Do: Intercepting Fist to cancel defense effects that might move the opponent away. Once their best blocks are gone, play Jeet Kune Do: Downward Side Kick to force a random discard, and finish with Momentous Shift or Jeet Kune Do: High Straight Lead for 5 damage. You can open your turn with Nunchaku (2 copies) to give all these attacks a +1 value.
**Watch out for:** A chain is easily broken. If the opponent plays a Feint, it cancels the action gain and ends your sequence. If they play a defense that moves them away, like Skirmish, you are left out of melee range and must waste an action to close the gap again. Do not hoard cards in hopes of a flawless six-attack sequence; throw smaller combinations to bait out their cancel cards.

### Plan B: Recycle your best moves

**Goal:** Replay the most impactful Jeet Kune Do cards to wear the opponent down.
**How to do it:** You only have one copy of each Jeet Kune Do card, but you can pull them from your discard pile. Be Like Water (4 copies, value 3) is a defense that returns one to your hand. "HOO! WHAAAAAA!" does the same thing on your turn as a scheme, and gives you an action so it is free to play. Use these to bring back High Straight Lead for heavy hits, or Downward Side Kick to repeatedly strip cards from their hand. To make this work, you need targets in your discard pile, so play a few Jeet Kune Do cards early in the game even if you cannot chain them.
**Watch out for:** Because your recursion is largely tied to a 3-value block, you will likely take chip damage to retrieve your cards. Furthermore, if an opponent refuses to attack you, you cannot trigger the defense at all.

### Plan C: Hit and step away

**Goal:** Force melee enemies to waste their actions chasing you.
**How to do it:** His special ability lets him move 1 space at the end of his turn. Always use this to step back after you finish attacking. A melee opponent who starts their turn one space away must spend their first action maneuvering toward you, leaving them with only one action to strike. This prevents them from hitting you twice in a single turn.
**Watch out for:** This evasion does little against ranged fighters, who can often shoot you without moving. It also fails if the map is too tight to back out of their reach.

### How they lose

- His 14 health is very low, and he has no healing. Every point of damage sticks.
- He has no sidekicks to absorb hits, block paths, or take pressure off his hero. Opponents can surround him with their own sidekicks to force him to waste cards and actions clearing a path.
- His defenses are relatively weak. Because most of his blocks cap at 3, attacks with a value of 4 or higher will consistently push damage through, and that chip damage quickly adds up to a defeat.
- Opponents who hold their Feints or movement effects specifically for his Jeet Kune Do cards shut down his aggressive turns.
- He gets pinned. With no sidekick to help him break out, getting trapped in a corner is a fast path to losing.

### Key cards

- **Bring It On**: A scheme that pulls an opposing fighter adjacent to you and refunds the action. It lets you catch ranged fighters without spending an action to maneuver, allowing you to start a chain immediately.
- **One-Inch Punch**: A scheme that deals 2 damage and returns to your hand if it defeats the target. It is perfect for repeatedly clearing 1-health sidekicks like Harpies or Outlaws without wasting attack cards.
- **Little Dragon**: A 2-value versatile that draws 2 cards after combat. If your hand is empty, its value becomes 6. Sources strongly disagree on this card: one calls it a dangerous trap because holding an empty hand is far too risky for a low-health fighter, while another notes it is an excellent tool for protecting yourself right after emptying your hand for a massive combo.

### Tips

- Combine Bring It On with Jeet Kune Do: High Straight Lead. The scheme moves the opponent, fulfilling the movement condition to guarantee the attack will hit for 5.
- Use Jeet Kune Do: Short Lead Hook to swap spaces and escape a pin, or to trigger your own Momentous Shift. Be careful, though: swapping spaces also activates your opponent's Momentous Shift if they play it on defense.
- Taste of Blood draws 3 cards, but only if you have 5 or less health. That is a dangerous health range for a 3-value block, so do not rely on it for card draw. If you draw it early, consider using its 3-boost value to maneuver.
- Nunchaku's damage boost is an effect on the scheme, not the attack, so a Feint cannot cancel it. The opponent will take the extra damage even if they stop the chain.
- Don't telegraph your biggest turns. If you play Nunchaku or Bring It On, opponents will immediately expect a chain and hold their Feints to disrupt it.
