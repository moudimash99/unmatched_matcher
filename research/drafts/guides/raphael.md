## Raphael

**In short:** An aggressive melee fighter with a high-health ranged sidekick. He attacks relentlessly to drain the opponent's hand, exploits his ability to gain extra actions by losing combats, and finishes with massive damage when their defenses are exhausted.

### Plan A: The extra action mind games

**Goal:** Gain an action by losing a combat, or punish an opponent who under-defends expecting a weak attack.
**How to do it:** His ability, Anger Issues, gives you an action the first time you lose a combat on your turn. Because the wording specifies "you", this applies to his sidekick, Casey Jones, as well. Lead with weak attacks that offer a benefit even in defeat. Let's do this! (value 1) draws a card. Crowd control (value 2) damages each adjacent opposing fighter and draws a card for each one. If the opponent commits a strong block to these, you lose the combat, reap the card draw, and recover your action to keep swinging.

Since opponents will anticipate this sequence, use it to bluff. Open with a heavier hit like Turtle power! (value 3) or Unbridled rage. If they absorb the blow expecting a weak bait card, they suffer heavy damage. If they defend Turtle power! with a high-value block, you can simply decline to use its optional boost, lose the combat, and pocket the extra action anyway.
**Watch out for:** You do not have to trigger your ability on every single turn. Sometimes the safest play is to use that bonus action to double maneuver, letting you draw cards and retreat rather than pushing a third attack.

### Plan B: Pressure their hand, then finish them

**Goal:** Whittle the opponent down to two or three cards, then land Unbridled rage for lethal damage.
**How to do it:** With three actions a turn and solid drawing tools, Raphael can maintain constant pressure. Utilize Casey's ranged attacks from a safe distance to burn the opponent's blocks without exposing Raphael to danger. Once their hand is depleted, unleash Unbridled rage. This 3-value attack allows you to discard any number of versatile and defense cards, adding +2 to the printed value for each. Chucking unused defensive tools can easily spike this attack to a 7 or 9.

You can also bait out their best defensive options with Casey's Slapshot. Normally a 2-value versatile, its value jumps to 6 if Casey is adjacent to the opposing fighter. Defenders must respect the threat of a 6 and will often burn a cancel or a premium block to stop it.
**Watch out for:** Emptying your hand to buff Unbridled rage leaves you completely exposed to a counter-attack. Always hold back at least one reliable block, such as Heroes in a half shell (value 5) or Relentless, to weather the opponent's next turn.

### Plan C: Recycle defenses

**Goal:** Stay alive while maintaining aggressive pressure.
**How to do it:** Raphael boasts a sturdy 17 health, but he relies heavily on his versatile cards for defense and lacks typical defensive card draw. Relentless (value 3) patches this weakness: after combat, it shuffles into your deck and draws a card. Use it primarily on defense. Attackers rarely cancel a 3-value block, allowing the effect to resolve, which keeps your deck thick and your hand stocked. If played during exhaustion when your deck is empty, it shuffles in and immediately draws itself right back.
**Watch out for:** If you attack with Relentless, the opponent is much more likely to cancel it, sending it to the discard pile permanently. Keep it reserved for blocking.

### How they lose

- Fast, aggressive fighters (like Chupacabra or Michelangelo) pressure him relentlessly, forcing him to burn through his limited defense cards.
- Because he lacks card draw on defensive cards outside of Relentless, being forced to double maneuver just to find blocks ruins his offensive tempo.
- A timely cancel on Relentless destroys his recursion engine, leaving him susceptible to late-game exhaustion.
- Overcommitting cards to Unbridled rage without securing the kill leaves him taking massive undefended damage on the following turn.

### Key cards

- **Unbridled rage**: Your ultimate finisher. Keep in mind that Casey's versatile cards are valid discard fodder for its boost effect, even if Casey has already been defeated.
- **Relentless**: Your premier block and sustainability engine.
- **Let's do this!**: The ideal opener. It lets you lose a combat safely while replacing itself in your hand, effortlessly triggering Anger Issues.
- **Payback time!**: A 2-value versatile that spikes to a 5 if you have lost a combat this turn. Deploy it as a heavy second attack after triggering your ability, or use it as an unbreakable 5-value block against fighters who attack you twice in one turn.
- **Slapshot**: Push Casey adjacent to force out cancels, or keep it as a bulky defense if an enemy traps him.
- **Break something**: A 2-value versatile that shuts down card effects. If nothing gets canceled, it guarantees 2 damage to an adjacent opposing fighter, making it excellent against blank cards or for chip damage even when losing the combat.
- **Batter up!**: Casey's 3-value attack. If it wins, it repositions the opponent up to three spaces, perfectly setting up Raphael's melee strikes.

### Tips

- Let Casey absorb the first combat loss of the turn. His ranged strikes safely activate Anger Issues, affording Raphael the bonus action needed to close the distance.
- Wait to see the defender's commitment before boosting Turtle power!. If they drop a massive block, keep your boost card in hand and accept the loss to gain an action instead.
- Be willing to absorb an undefended attack early in the match if it preserves your versatile cards or Heroes in a half shell for a lethal Unbridled rage later.
- Raphael is cool but rude deals 1 damage to opposing fighters adjacent to *at least one* friendly fighter. Grouping both Raphael and Casey next to a single enemy will not double the damage.
- Use Casey's 8 health to physically block choke points and protect Raphael after he dumps his hand for a heavy attack.
