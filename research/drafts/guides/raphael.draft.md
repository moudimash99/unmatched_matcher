## Raphael

**In short:** An aggressive melee fighter with a high-health ranged sidekick. He attacks relentlessly to drain the opponent's hand, uses his ability to gain extra actions by losing combats, and finishes with massive damage when their defenses are gone.

### Plan A: The extra action mind games

**Goal:** Gain an action by losing a combat, or punish an opponent who under-defends expecting a weak attack.
**How to do it:** His ability, Anger Issues, gives you an action the first time you lose a combat on your turn. Because the ability says "you", it applies to Casey Jones as well. Lead with weak attacks that give you a benefit even if you lose. Let's do this! (3 copies, value 1) draws a card. Crowd control (3 copies, value 2) deals 1 damage to each adjacent enemy and draws a card for each. If the opponent plays a big defense, you lose the combat, get the card draw, and gain your action back to attack again.

Because opponents expect this loop, you can bluff. Attack first with Turtle power! (value 3) or Unbridled rage (value 3). If they take the hit expecting a 1-value bait card, they take massive damage. If they defend Turtle power! heavily, you can choose not to boost it, lose the combat, and still get your action.
**Watch out for:** Don't force yourself to gain an action every turn. Sometimes the right move is to use that gained action to double maneuver away instead of attacking a third time, so you can draw cards and stay safe.

### Plan B: Pressure their hand, then finish them

**Goal:** Whittle the opponent down to two or three cards, then land Unbridled rage for lethal damage.
**How to do it:** With three actions a turn and huge card draw from his effects, Raphael can attack constantly to keep the opponent on the back foot. Use Casey's ranged attacks from a safe distance to force out blocks without putting Raphael in danger. Once their hand is low, drop Unbridled rage. This attack lets you discard any number of versatile and defense cards to add +2 to its value for each. With a hand full of unused defenses, this easily reaches 7 or 9.

You can also force out cancels using Casey's Slapshot. It is a 2-value versatile, but if Casey is adjacent to the opposing fighter, its value becomes 6. Opponents have to respect a 6 and will often spend a Feint or their best block on it.
**Watch out for:** Discarding your whole hand to boost Unbridled rage leaves you helpless on the opponent's next turn. Always keep at least one strong block, like Heroes in a half shell or Relentless, to survive the counter-attack.

### Plan C: Recycle defenses

**Goal:** Stay alive while maintaining aggressive pressure.
**How to do it:** Raphael has 17 health, but he lacks basic defensive card draw. Relentless (2 copies, value 3) solves this: after combat, it shuffles itself back into your deck and draws a card. Play it on defense whenever possible. Attackers rarely cancel a 3-value defense, so it almost always resolves, keeping your deck thick and your hand full. If you play it while facing the exhaustion clock with an empty deck, it shuffles in and immediately draws itself back to your hand.
**Watch out for:** If you play Relentless on attack, the opponent is much more likely to Feint it, sending it to the discard pile forever. Save it to block.

### How they lose

​- Fast, aggressive fighters (like Chupacabra or Michelangelo) pressure him constantly, forcing him to spend his limited defense cards.
​- He has no defensive card draw outside of Relentless. If he is forced to double maneuver just to find blocks, he loses his tempo.
​- A Feint on Relentless breaks his recursion loop and leaves him vulnerable to exhaustion.
​- He discards too many cards for Unbridled rage, fails to kill the opponent, and takes massive undefended damage on the next turn.

### Key cards

​- **Unbridled rage**: his finisher. A 3-value attack that gets +2 for every defense or versatile card you discard. Wait until their hand is mostly empty before using it. Casey's versatile cards count for this, even if Casey is defeated.
​- **Relentless**: a 3-value versatile that shuffles itself back and draws a card. Your best defense, and your engine to keep fighting.
​- **Let's do this!**: a 1-value attack that draws a card, or two if you win. The perfect opener to lose combat safely, replenish your hand, and trigger Anger Issues.
​- **Payback time!**: a 2-value versatile that becomes a 5 if you lost a combat this turn. Use it as your second attack after triggering your ability, or as a strong 5-value block against a fighter who attacks twice.
​- **Slapshot**: a 2-value versatile for Casey. Use it aggressively when adjacent to bait out Feints, or save it for defense if a melee fighter corners him.
​- **Break something**: a 2-value versatile that cancels effects or deals 2 damage if nothing was canceled. Good for guaranteed damage against cards with no effects, and it hits an adjacent enemy even if you lose the combat.

### Tips

​- Let Casey take the first loss of the turn. His attacks can safely trigger Anger Issues from range, giving Raphael the extra action to move in.
​- Turtle power! says you *may* boost it. Wait to see the opponent's defense before deciding. If they play a high-value block, save your boost card and take the loss to gain an action.
​- Don't be afraid to take an undefended hit early if it means saving your Heroes in a half shell or versatile cards to fuel a massive Unbridled rage later.
​- Use Raphael is cool but rude to clear out 1-health sidekicks or deal unblockable area damage when fighters cluster together.
​- Use Casey for body blocking to protect Raphael once he dumps his defenses.
